Serving Wine: Glasses, Decanting and Storing

Glasses, decanting, storing. 7 lessons · about 54 min

Start with the free units See prices

This unit is included with Premium ($3.99 a month, 3 days free) or the course for life ($39.99 once).

The 7 lessons

  1. 1

    How temperature changes a wine

    Serving temperature turns a wine's smells and taste up or down, so chilling whites and cooling reds slightly helps each one show its best.

    You will be able to:

    • explain how cold and warmth change a wine's taste
    • order wine styles from coldest to warmest serving
    • use a fridge and bucket to serve wine better
    about 7 min
  2. 2

    Why glass shape matters, and the glasses you need

    A glass's bowl and rim steer how a wine's smells reach your nose, which is why shape matters and why two good glasses cover almost every wine.

    You will be able to:

    • explain how a glass's shape changes what you taste
    • match reds, whites and sparkling wine to the right glass
    • choose the two glasses that cover almost every wine
    about 8 min
  3. 3

    How much to pour, and in what order

    Pour about a third of a glass (roughly 150 ml) so the wine has room to swirl, and when tasting several, go from lighter to bolder so nothing gets overpowered.

    You will be able to:

    • pour about 150 ml, roughly a third of a glass
    • explain why empty space in the glass helps
    • order several wines from gentle to bold
    about 7 min
  4. 4

    Opening bottles: corks, screwcaps and bubbles

    Steady hands beat strength: twist the bottle for a screwcap, pull a cork in two calm steps, rescue a crumbling one, and ease bubbles out with a sigh.

    You will be able to:

    • open a screwcap and a cork calmly and cleanly
    • rescue a crumbling or broken cork with the right tool
    • open sparkling wine safely, with a sigh not a pop
    about 8 min
  5. 5

    Decanting: why, when and how to do it

    Decanting means pouring wine into another container for one of two reasons: to give a young, harsh red some air so it softens, or to leave an old red's gritty sediment behind.

    You will be able to:

    • soften a harsh young red by splashing it
    • pour an old red gently off its sediment
    • spot the wines that need no decanting
    about 8 min
  6. 6

    Keeping an open bottle fresh for days

    Why air slowly tires an open wine, how a cork and the fridge buy you 3 to 5 days, which wines fade fastest, and how to keep bubbles fizzy.

    You will be able to:

    • keep an open still wine good for days
    • explain why air wears an open wine down
    • spot fragile wines and keep sparkling wine fizzy
    about 8 min
  7. 7

    Storing wine at home: cool, dark, still and soon

    Why wine fears heat, swings, light and shaking, where to keep bottles at home, why corks lie down, and why most wine is best opened soon.

    You will be able to:

    • name the four enemies of wine at home
    • choose a good spot and position for bottles
    • tell an everyday bottle from one worth keeping
    about 8 min
