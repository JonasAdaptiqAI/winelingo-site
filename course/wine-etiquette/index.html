Wine at the Table: Restaurants and Etiquette

Restaurants, etiquette, parties and gifts. 11 lessons · about 86 min

Start with the free units See prices

This unit is included with Premium ($3.99 a month, 3 days free) or the course for life ($39.99 once).

The 11 lessons

  1. 1

    Ordering wine in a restaurant with confidence

    What to tell the sommelier, what the bottle ritual is for, and when sending a wine back is fair: faulty or wrong, not just unfamiliar.

    You will be able to:

    • ask for help and name your budget with confidence
    • check the bottle and the first taste for faults
    • send back a faulty or wrong bottle politely
    about 8 min
  2. 2

    Is this wine faulty? A step-by-step check at home

    A simple routine for a suspect bottle: look, sniff, taste and give it air, plus the faults air fixes, two faults that show after you swallow, and how to return a bad bottle.

    You will be able to:

    • run a four-step check on a suspect bottle
    • sort faults into those air fixes and those it cannot
    • recognise smoke taint and mousiness in the aftertaste
    about 8 min
  3. 3

    Wine etiquette without the snobbery

    Why we toast, who is poured first, how to say no thanks and what to do with a wine you dislike: wine manners are care for people, not clever words.

    You will be able to:

    • show good wine manners without any fancy words
    • serve, toast and decline a top-up with confidence
    • handle a wine you dislike kindly at a friend's
    about 7 min
  4. 4

    Hosting a wine tasting at home

    Pick a theme, pour four to six wines from light to full, and make the evening about talking, not about being right.

    You will be able to:

    • choose a theme and four to six wines
    • plan tasting pours and set a simple table
    • run a single or double blind tasting with a reveal
    about 8 min
  5. 5

    How much wine for a party: the sums and the logistics

    Count wine for a dinner or a drinks party, stretch sparkling for a toast, buy on sale or return, chill a crowd's worth and get everyone home safely.

    You will be able to:

    • work out bottles for a dinner or a drinks party
    • plan the mix, the chilling and sale or return
    • plan water, food and a safe way home for guests
    about 8 min
  6. 6

    Choosing wine as a gift: start with the person

    The best wine gift starts with what the person already enjoys, not the price tag: safe bets, story bottles, gifts for non-drinkers and a good note.

    You will be able to:

    • choose a wine gift around what the person enjoys
    • name safe bets and wines to skip as gifts
    • give a bottle with a note and no pressure
    about 8 min
  7. 7

    The service sequence: from bottle to glass

    For new staff: show the bottle, open it at the table, let the host taste, pour in the right order, then look after the bottle and the top-ups.

    You will be able to:

    • present a bottle to the host with name and vintage
    • pour for a table in the right order, without drips
    • serve sparkling wine and use an ice bucket well
    about 8 min
  8. 8

    Describe any wine to a guest in one sentence

    A simple four-part sentence (style, feel, one flavour, one dish) that lets you describe any wine in plain words a guest can picture.

    You will be able to:

    • describe any wine in one four-part sentence
    • replace wine jargon with words guests understand
    • answer honestly about a wine you have not tasted
    about 7 min
  9. 9

    Recommend by the glass and by budget

    Two questions, three bottles at three prices, a quiet way to talk about money, and the glass as a safe way for a guest to try.

    You will be able to:

    • ask two questions that lead to the right wine
    • offer three bottles at three prices with confidence
    • suggest a step up or a glass without pushing
    about 8 min
  10. 10

    When a guest sends a bottle back

    For new staff: tell a faulty bottle from one a guest simply dislikes, check it calmly, say the right words and know where the bottle goes next.

    You will be able to:

    • tell a faulty bottle from one simply not liked
    • check a returned wine calmly and say the right words
    • handle a returned bottle so nothing goes to waste
    about 8 min
  11. 11

    Responsible service: water, limits and when to stop

    Know what a glass holds, offer water as hospitality, spot the signs a guest has had enough, and slow down or refuse kindly.

    You will be able to:

    • explain what a unit or genstand holds in a glass
    • spot the signs that a guest has had enough
    • slow service and refuse more alcohol kindly
    about 8 min
