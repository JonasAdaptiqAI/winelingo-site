In the Vineyard: How Wine Grapes Are Grown

How the grapes are grown. 6 lessons · about 48 min

Start with the free units See prices

This unit is included with Premium ($3.99 a month, 3 days free) or the course for life ($39.99 once).

The 6 lessons

  1. 1

    The vine's year, from bud to harvest

    A grapevine repeats the same six stages every year, from budburst to winter sleep, and where it is in that cycle decides what the grower is doing.

    You will be able to:

    • name the six stages of a vine's year in order
    • explain why flowering decides the size of the crop
    • read the season in a vineyard, north or south
    about 8 min
  2. 2

    Why most vines are two plants

    Most vines are a European variety grafted onto American roots: why those roots survive a root-eating insect, how grafting works and how growers pick one.

    You will be able to:

    • explain why most vines are two grafted plants
    • describe how American roots survive phylloxera
    • name what growers weigh when choosing a rootstock
    about 8 min
  3. 3

    Fewer grapes, better grapes: how growers steer quality

    Cutting vines back in winter, managing leaves in summer and spacing vines at planting: three ways to get fewer, riper, tastier grapes per vine.

    You will be able to:

    • explain why growers cut away most of last year's growth
    • describe three tools growers use to limit the crop
    • explain what shade and heavy crops do to wine
    about 8 min
  4. 4

    What goes wrong in the vineyard, and why vintages differ

    Frost, hail, heat, mould and rot: how one year's weather decides the crop, and why the same vineyard makes a different wine every year.

    You will be able to:

    • explain why the same vineyard's wine differs by vintage
    • describe what frost, hail and heat do to grapes
    • explain when botrytis becomes grey rot or noble rot
    about 8 min
  5. 5

    Harvest day: when to pick, and by hand or machine

    Why harvest day is the grower's hardest call, with sugar, acidity and flavour on different clocks, and how the grapes come in: by hand or by machine.

    You will be able to:

    • explain why sugar, acidity and flavour rarely peak together
    • predict how picking early or late shapes a wine
    • compare hand-picking and machine harvesting for quality and cost
    about 8 min
  6. 6

    Organic, biodynamic, natural, sustainable: what they promise

    Four label words describe how a wine is grown or made, not how good it tastes: what organic, biodynamic, natural and sustainable really promise.

    You will be able to:

    • explain what organic, biodynamic, natural and sustainable promise
    • read organic wine labels from the EU and US
    • tell a farming word from a cellar word
    about 8 min
