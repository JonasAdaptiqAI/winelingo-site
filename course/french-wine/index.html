French Wine Course

Bordeaux, Burgundy, Rhône, Loire and Champagne. 10 lessons · about 77 min

The 10 lessons

  1. 1

    France at a glance: terroir, climate and grapes

    Why France names its wines after places: terroir, three climates from cool north to warm south, the soils, the signature grapes and the history behind them.

    You will be able to:

    • explain terroir and its four parts to a friend
    • match France's classic grapes to their home regions
    • describe France's three climates and what they do
    about 8 min
  2. 2

    The grapes of France: this place, this grape

    French labels name places, not grapes. Learn the code: which grape each famous place means, from Bordeaux and Burgundy to Sancerre, Vouvray and Champagne.

    You will be able to:

    • name the grape behind France's most famous place names
    • explain how an appellation ties a place to grapes
    • find a French wine made from a grape you like
    about 7 min
  3. 3

    Bordeaux: rivers, banks and the famous châteaux

    How two rivers split Bordeaux into gravel and clay, Cabernet and Merlot; what a château, the 1855 ranking and en primeur mean; and the dry and sweet whites.

    You will be able to:

    • explain how Bordeaux's rivers and soils shape its reds
    • read a Bordeaux label for its bank, grape and rank
    • name Bordeaux's white styles and explain en primeur
    about 8 min
  4. 4

    Burgundy: two grapes and a map of villages

    Burgundy makes reds from Pinot Noir and whites from Chardonnay, so the map does the talking: slopes, villages and named plots, where the narrower the place, the higher the rank.

    You will be able to:

    • name Burgundy's main areas and what each one makes
    • zoom from Bourgogne to Grand Cru on the map
    • explain why Burgundy's vineyards are split into tiny plots
    about 8 min
  5. 5

    The Rhône: Syrah in the north, blends in the south

    The Rhône has two halves: steep northern slopes make peppery Syrah, while the hot, windswept south makes Grenache-led blends. Learn the names that tell them apart.

    You will be able to:

    • tell northern Syrah reds from southern Grenache blends
    • name the Rhône's key crus and what they make
    • climb the Côtes du Rhône ladder from base to cru
    about 8 min
  6. 6

    The Loire and Alsace: France's white heartlands

    Two cool corners of France for crisp whites: in the Loire, the place points to the grape; in Alsace, the label names it.

    You will be able to:

    • place the Loire's four zones and their main grapes
    • explain how the Vosges rain shadow helps Alsace
    • recognise an Alsace wine by its label and bottle
    about 8 min
  7. 7

    The south: Languedoc, Provence and rosé

    Pale, dry Provence rosé and the sunny, good-value Languedoc: what is in the bottle, how to read the label and how to pick one.

    You will be able to:

    • explain why Provence rosé is pale and dry
    • name the Languedoc's main red grapes and its oyster white
    • pick a southern French wine by style and label
    about 8 min
  8. 8

    Champagne and its cousins: how the bubbles are made

    Champagne is sparkling wine from one cool, chalky French region, where yeast working a second time inside the bottle makes the bubbles and long ageing adds toasty notes.

    You will be able to:

    • explain how the traditional method puts bubbles in a bottle
    • tell vintage from non-vintage, and Brut from Extra Dry
    • tell Champagne, Crémant, Cava and Prosecco apart
    about 7 min
  9. 9

    Read a French label: the place comes first

    Put what you know about French wine to work on a real bottle: turn the place into a region and a grape, then read the tier and the maker.

    You will be able to:

    • turn a French place name into a region and grape
    • tell estate words from free words on a bottle
    • decode a French label, line by line
    about 7 min
  10. 10

    Choosing French wine: good value by region

    For each main French region, the safe everyday bottle and the step up, plus how to read price against the ladder of place names.

    You will be able to:

    • pick a safe everyday bottle from each French region
    • step up one rung when you want more
    • swap a famous name for a good-value neighbour
    about 8 min
