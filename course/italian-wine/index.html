Italian Wine Course

Piedmont, Tuscany, Veneto and the south. 8 lessons · about 60 min

Start with the free units See prices

This unit is included with Premium ($3.99 a month, 3 days free) or the course for life ($39.99 once).

The 8 lessons

  1. 1

    Italy at a glance: native grapes and wine for the table

    Why Italian wine tastes the way it does: hundreds of native grapes, fresh acidity made for food, and a long, hilly land from the cool Alps to the hot south.

    You will be able to:

    • name the three traits that make Italian wine recognisable
    • explain how hills, height and sea keep Italian grapes fresh
    • explain why each part of Italy has its own grapes
    about 7 min
  2. 2

    The grapes of Italy: the few behind the famous names

    Most famous Italian wines name a place, not a grape. Learn the handful behind them: Sangiovese, Nebbiolo, Barbera, the southern reds and four key whites.

    You will be able to:

    • name the grapes behind Chianti, Barolo, Prosecco and Soave
    • tell Sangiovese, Nebbiolo and Barbera apart by tannin
    • explain why Vino Nobile is not Montepulciano grape wine
    about 8 min
  3. 3

    Piedmont: Barolo, Barbaresco and Barbera

    Piedmont's great reds, Barolo and Barbaresco, are pure Nebbiolo: pale, drying and built to age, while Dolcetto and Barbera cover everyday drinking.

    You will be able to:

    • locate Piedmont's best vineyards in the Langhe hills
    • describe Barolo and Barbaresco and why they need time
    • tell Piedmont's everyday reds from its long-lived ones
    about 7 min
  4. 4

    Tuscany: Chianti, Brunello and the Super Tuscans

    Tuscany's famous reds are built on one grape, Sangiovese, and the label tells you which hills it grew on, how long it aged and whether the maker broke the rules.

    You will be able to:

    • read a Tuscan label for place, ageing and rules
    • tell Chianti Classico, Brunello and Vino Nobile apart
    • explain what Super Tuscans are and why they matter
    about 7 min
  5. 5

    Veneto and the north-east: Prosecco, Amarone and Soave

    How Italy's cool north-east turns a few grapes into very different wines: Prosecco made fizzy in big tanks, Amarone from dried grapes and fresh white Soave.

    You will be able to:

    • explain how Prosecco gets its bubbles
    • read Prosecco's quality steps and sweetness words
    • describe Valpolicella, Ripasso, Amarone and Soave in a line each
    about 7 min
  6. 6

    The south and the islands: sun, volcanoes and value

    Italy's hot south and islands make ripe, soft reds at great value, while height, sea breezes and volcanic soil keep the best wines, such as Etna's, fresh.

    You will be able to:

    • explain how the hot south still makes fresh wines
    • name the key grapes of Puglia, Campania and the islands
    • tell Etna's fresh reds from the south's riper ones
    about 8 min
  7. 7

    Read an Italian label: quality levels and key words

    How to read a real Italian bottle: the four facts on the front, the quality level in small print, invented names, and the words for place, time and style.

    You will be able to:

    • find the four key facts on an Italian front label
    • decode a quality level written out in Italian
    • read place, ageing and style words on a real bottle
    about 8 min
  8. 8

    Choosing Italian wine: the right bottle for the moment

    North, Tuscany and the south side by side: which Italian bottle to pick for the mood, the dish and the budget.

    You will be able to:

    • choose an Italian red by style: firm, tangy or ripe
    • match Italian wines to tomato, meat, fish and dessert
    • pick younger siblings and everyday bottles for value
    about 8 min
