Argentinian Wine Course

Malbec, Mendoza, Salta and Patagonia. 6 lessons · about 45 min

Start with the free units See prices

The 6 lessons

  1. 1

    Argentina at a glance: Malbec, mountains and desert sun

    Argentina is the land of soft, plush Malbec, grown in near-desert vineyards high on the lower slopes of the Andes, where melted snow waters the vines and cool nights keep the wine fresh.

    You will be able to:

    • describe the plush style of Argentine Malbec
    • explain how snowmelt and altitude make a desert work
    • place Mendoza, San Juan, Salta and Patagonia on the map
    about 7 min
  2. 2

    The grapes of Argentina: Malbec and more

    Malbec leads Argentina's grapes, with Bonarda and the Cabernets behind it, flowery Torrontés as the star white and old Criolla grapes for cheap everyday wine.

    You will be able to:

    • describe how Argentine Malbec tastes and why it nearly vanished
    • name Argentina's supporting reds and what each one brings
    • tell perfumed Torrontés apart from the everyday Criolla grapes
    about 8 min
  3. 3

    Mendoza: desert, snowmelt and Malbec at altitude

    Mendoza is a high desert watered by melted Andes snow: sunny days and cool nights give soft, plush Malbec near the city and fresher, more flowery Malbec up in the Uco Valley.

    You will be able to:

    • explain how snowmelt channels water a desert
    • explain why altitude makes Malbec fresher and deeper
    • tell plush Luján de Cuyo from fresh Uco Valley
    about 7 min
  4. 4

    Salta and Patagonia: high north, cool south

    Argentina's two wine extremes: Salta's sky-high, sunny valleys make perfumed Torrontés and deep reds, while Patagonia's cool, windy river valleys make fresh Pinot Noir.

    You will be able to:

    • explain why Salta and Patagonia both make fresh wines
    • describe Salta's Torrontés, Malbec and Tannat
    • explain why Patagonia suits fresh Pinot Noir
    about 8 min
  5. 5

    Reading an Argentine label: place, grape, ageing

    Read an Argentine bottle like a local: the IP, IG and DOC place levels, small districts like Paraje Altamira, the 85 per cent rule, Reserva and altitude.

    You will be able to:

    • tell Argentina's IP, IG and DOC place levels apart
    • apply the 85 per cent rule and Reserva minimums
    • read an Argentine label from front to back
    about 8 min
  6. 6

    Choosing Argentine wine: Malbec and beyond

    A buyer's guide to Argentina: everyday Malbec or the Uco step up, what a height on the label means, other reds, Torrontés, Pinot Noir and value.

    You will be able to:

    • choose between everyday Mendoza and Uco Valley Malbec
    • read a vineyard height on an Argentine label
    • pick Argentine wines beyond Malbec for food and value
    about 7 min
