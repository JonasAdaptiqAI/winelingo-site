Wine Tasting Course: How to Taste Wine

How to taste, step by step. 10 lessons · about 79 min · Free

Start this unit free

The 10 lessons

  1. 1

    Wine 101: the main styles of wine and how to read them

    Wine is grape juice fermented by yeast, and almost every bottle belongs to a few families you can read by colour, sweetness, bubbles and strength.

    You will be able to:

    • explain how yeast turns grape juice into wine
    • tell still, sparkling, sweet and fortified wines apart
    • read any wine by colour, sweetness, bubbles and strength
    about 8 min
  2. 2

    How to taste wine in 5 steps

    Tasting is just drinking, slowed down: See, Swirl, Smell, Sip and Savour, five steps that give each sense its turn and help you notice what you like.

    You will be able to:

    • name the five steps of tasting wine in order
    • explain why smell matters more than your tongue
    • notice and describe a wine's finish after swallowing
    about 8 min
  3. 3

    Body: what light and full really mean

    Body is the weight and texture of a wine in your mouth, from light to full, driven mainly by alcohol and extract and easily pictured as skim milk to cream.

    You will be able to:

    • explain what body means using the milk picture
    • name the two main things that add body
    • tell light-bodied from full-bodied wines by feel
    about 8 min
  4. 4

    Tannin: the grippy, drying feel in red wine

    Tannin is a feeling, not a flavour: where the drying grip comes from, why some grapes have more, how to judge it, and how time and food soften it.

    You will be able to:

    • recognise tannin as a drying, grippy feeling
    • explain where tannin comes from and why reds have more
    • choose food that softens a tannic red
    about 8 min
  5. 5

    Acidity: why your mouth waters

    How to recognise acidity, the tart, mouth-watering freshness that keeps wine lively and food-friendly, and how to sense it without trusting any single clue.

    You will be able to:

    • recognise acidity as wine's tart, mouth-watering freshness
    • spot acidity using several clues, not just one
    • explain why acidity makes wine good with food
    about 8 min
  6. 6

    Sweet or dry? How to taste the difference

    Sweetness is sugar the yeast left behind. Learn the dry to sweet scale, the label words, and why a fruity smell can fool your nose.

    You will be able to:

    • explain how leftover sugar makes a wine sweet
    • place a wine on the dry to sweet scale
    • judge sweetness by taste, not by fruity smell
    about 8 min
  7. 7

    The aroma wheel: naming what you smell

    Name a wine's smell the easy way: pick a broad family first, then step out to the exact note, and learn the few smells that mean a wine is faulty.

    You will be able to:

    • sort a wine's smell into a broad family
    • spot the notes that point to oak
    • tell a personal impression from a wine fault
    about 8 min
  8. 8

    Primary, secondary, tertiary: where wine smells come from

    A wine's smells come in three layers: from the grape, from winemaking and from age. Sort them and you can read a wine's style, and guess its age.

    You will be able to:

    • sort wine smells into primary, secondary and tertiary
    • explain where each group of aromas comes from
    • use aromas as careful clues to a wine's age
    about 8 min
  9. 9

    How temperature changes a wine

    Serving temperature turns a wine's smells and taste up or down, so chilling whites well and cooling reds slightly helps each one show its best.

    You will be able to:

    • explain how cold and warmth change a wine's taste
    • order wine styles from coldest to warmest serving
    • use a fridge and bucket to serve wine better
    about 7 min
  10. 10

    Building your palate: how anyone learns to taste

    Tasting is a skill anyone can build: attentive side-by-side practice, a memory bank of everyday smells and honest notes that map your taste.

    You will be able to:

    • explain why a great palate is built, not born
    • set up a side-by-side flight and a smell bank
    • keep honest notes that reveal what you enjoy
    about 8 min
