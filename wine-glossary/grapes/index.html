Wine glossary: Grapes

Grapes terms

Grape variety
The specific type of grape a wine is made from, such as Merlot or Chardonnay. Sometimes called the ‘varietal’. It’s the single biggest clue to how a wine will taste.
Cabernet Sauvignon
A widely planted red grape making bold, structured wines with firm tannin and blackcurrant flavours. A cornerstone of Bordeaux and popular worldwide.
Merlot
A red grape making softer, rounder, plummy wines with gentler tannin. Approachable on its own and a common blending partner for Cabernet Sauvignon.
Pinot Noir
A lighter-bodied red grape known for red-berry fruit, silky texture and delicacy. Famous in Burgundy and prized for its finesse.
Chardonnay
A versatile white grape that can be crisp and unoaked or rich and buttery depending on how it’s made. Grown almost everywhere wine is made.
Sauvignon Blanc
An aromatic white grape making zesty, refreshing wines with grassy, citrus and green notes. Loved for its bright, mouth-watering acidity.
Riesling
An aromatic white grape making wines that range from bone-dry to lusciously sweet, always with vivid acidity and floral, citrus and stone-fruit notes.
