Winelingo alternatives

Winelingo is not the right app for everyone. Here is when another one fits better, written by the people who make it.

Last checked 10 November 2026

Who wrote this. Winelingo did, and Winelingo is one of the apps on this page, so read it with that in mind. Facts about other apps come from their App Store listings and their own websites, with the sources linked under each table, and were checked on 10 November 2026. We did not install and test every app. Found something wrong or out of date? Tell us and we will fix it.

When another app is the better choice

  • You want a course that is completely free

    Wine Master: Learn Wine. Its listing says it is free with no ads or in-app purchases, with more than 460 short lessons in 14 languages and WSET Level 1 to 3 practice. In Winelingo's iPhone app every lesson is open too, but with hearts that run out when you make mistakes; unlimited hearts are part of Premium.

  • You have an Android phone and want an app

    Wine Master, Cork Dork or Vivino. Winelingo has no Android app. Its course and tests run in a browser on Android, but scanning needs an iPhone.

  • You want ratings and prices for a bottle

    Vivino for crowd ratings, Wine-Searcher for prices. Winelingo has very few ratings from other users, no public reviews and no prices. Winelingo vs Vivino.

  • You want to manage a cellar

    CellarTracker. Winelingo keeps a tasting journal, not an inventory.

  • You want exam flashcards

    Cork Dork Wine Study. It is built around flashcards and map quizzes for WSET Level 2 and 3 and the Court of Master Sommeliers Introductory and Certified exams. Winelingo has free practice tests, but no flashcard decks.

  • You want detailed wine maps

    Wine Folly Plus. Zoomable maps and infographics. It starts free; the full collection is $99.99 a year (App Store listing).

  • You want scanning, lessons and a cellar in one app

    SomeWine. A new iPhone app with a 32-lesson course, a scanner and a cellar. Its free plan allows 10 label scans in total; Winelingo's allows 5 a month.

When Winelingo is the right choice

  • You want to learn wine in short daily lessons and also understand the bottle or the wine list in front of you, in one app.
  • You want the scan to explain the wine (body, tannin, acidity, sweetness and why), and to pick bottles from a wine list that match your own palate.
  • You run a restaurant or wine bar and want your team trained on your own wine list. See Winelingo for restaurants.
  • You want an app with no wine shop and no ads.

What to know before you choose Winelingo

  • It is new (August 2026) and made by one independent developer.
  • There is no Android app.
  • Its wine database is small next to apps that have collected labels for more than a decade, so a scan of a rare bottle can miss.
  • Free lessons use hearts; free scans are limited to 5 a month. Prices.

FAQ

Questions & answers

What are the best alternatives to Winelingo?

For a completely free wine course: Wine Master. For crowd ratings: Vivino. For prices: Wine-Searcher. For managing a cellar: CellarTracker. For exam flashcards: Cork Dork. For scanning, lessons and a cellar in one iPhone app: SomeWine.

Is there a free alternative to Winelingo?

Wine Master's App Store listing says it is free with no ads or in-app purchases, and it has more than 460 lessons. Winelingo itself is free to start: every lesson is open in the iPhone app, with hearts that refill over time, and the first two course units and all practice tests are free on the web.

Is there a Winelingo alternative for Android?

Yes. Wine Master and Cork Dork have Android apps for learning, and Vivino, Wine-Searcher and CellarTracker have Android apps for ratings, prices and cellars. Winelingo's course and tests also run in the browser on Android.
