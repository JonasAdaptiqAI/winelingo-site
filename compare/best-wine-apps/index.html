Apps that teach you wine

These apps are built to teach. They differ in how: Winelingo and Wine Master are step-by-step courses, Decanter Know Your Wine drills facts with repeated questions, True Wine uses short game-style lessons, and SomeWine combines a short course with scanning and a cellar.

  • Winelingo

    iPhone, and the course in any browser · free, Premium $3.99 a month

    Short lessons, games and practice tests, plus a scanner that explains a label or a wine list in plain words. It is our app. It is new and small: no Android app, very few ratings from other users, and a much smaller wine database than Vivino.

  • Wine Master: Learn Wine

    iPhone, Android, Web · free

    The listing describes 28 "islands" and more than 460 short lessons in 14 languages, with WSET Level 1 to 3 practice, and says it is free with no ads or in-app purchases. The iPhone listing says its camera does not identify wines or scan labels; the Android listing describes a scan that reads the words on a label without looking the wine up in a database. First released in September 2026.

  • SomeWine

    iPhone · free, Pro $4.99 a month

    A new app (July 2026) that puts a 32-lesson course, label scanning, a cellar and food pairing in one place. The free plan has 10 label scans in total and a 3-bottle cellar; wine-list scanning needs Pro.

  • Decanter Know Your Wine

    iPhone · over 10 free modules, then $19.99 a year or $0.99 a module

    From the wine magazine Decanter. It teaches with modules of questions and spaced repetition, so it suits memorising facts. The iPhone app was last updated in September 2024.

  • True Wine

    iPhone · free with ads, Premium $9.99 a month or $59.99 a year

    Game-style lessons and a map of wine regions. The app was last updated in March 2025.

Four more learning apps turned up in our research and are not compared here: Vinoh (a wine journal with guided lessons), Winemind (micro-courses and quizzes), Duo Vino (lessons, flashcards and WSET Level 2 mock exams) and Wine Trainer (flashcard questions for WSET and sommelier study). WSET's own app, WSET Tasting Notes, is a tasting-note tool, not a course.

Apps for the bottle in front of you

These are the apps most people mean by "wine app": point the camera at a label and get an answer. They answer different questions, though. Vivino answers "is it good?", Wine-Searcher answers "what should it cost?", CellarTracker answers "what do I own?".

  • Vivino

    iPhone, Android, Web · free, Premium from $4.99

    The biggest, by its own figures. Scan a label for a crowd rating, reviews, tasting notes and food pairings, and buy from partner merchants in the app. Its listing describes no lessons or quizzes. Vivino lists the restaurant wine-list scanner as a Premium benefit.

  • Wine-Searcher

    iPhone, Android, Web · free, PRO paid

    A price-comparison engine. It finds which merchants stock a wine and at what price, then sends you to the merchant to buy.

  • CellarTracker

    iPhone, Android, Web · free tier, $40 to $500 a year by cellar size

    The collector's tool: inventory, community tasting notes, values and drinking windows. Prices, valuation and the AI features need a subscription. Its help pages list wine-list scanning under Beta.

  • Delectable

    iPhone, Android, Web · free with ads, Premium $5.99 a month

    A photo journal with a feed of what wine professionals drink; it is owned by the wine publication Vinous. Check before you rely on it: the iPhone app was last updated in September 2021.

Apps for exam study

  • Cork Dork Wine Study

    iPhone, Android · six study categories free, Pro from $7.99 a month

    Flashcards, map quizzes and practice questions aimed at WSET Level 2 and 3 and the Court of Master Sommeliers Introductory and Certified exams. Source: its App Store listing.

  • Wine Folly Plus

    iPhone, Android, Web · starts free, the full collection $99.99 a year

    Zoomable maps and infographics from Wine Folly. Reference material more than a course: Wine Folly's courses are sold separately. Source: its App Store listing and winefolly.com/plus.

  • Winelingo's practice tests

    Any browser · free

    Unofficial practice for WSET Level 1 to 3 and the Introductory Sommelier exam, including a timed 50-question Level 2 mock exam. No app needed.

Cork Dork and Winelingo both state that they are not affiliated with WSET or the Court of Master Sommeliers. No app replaces the course itself.

One older app to know about

Hello Vino still appears on many lists. It suggests wine for a dish or an occasion and is free, but its iPhone app was last updated in September 2017, according to its App Store listing.

How to choose

You do not have to pick one. A common pairing is a ratings app for the shop shelf and a learning app for the rest of the week: the first tells you what other people thought, the second teaches you why you like what you like, so you need the first one less over time.

FAQ

Questions & answers

What is the best wine app in 2026?

There is no single best one, because wine apps do different jobs. Vivino is the largest for ratings and buying. Wine-Searcher is the one for prices. CellarTracker is the one for managing a cellar. For learning wine, Winelingo and Wine Master are step-by-step courses.

What is the best app to learn about wine?

Winelingo and Wine Master both teach wine step by step in short lessons. Winelingo adds games, a scanner that explains the bottle or wine list in front of you, and free WSET-style practice tests; it is free to start, with Premium at $3.99 a month. Wine Master's listing says it is free with no in-app purchases and has more than 460 lessons; its iPhone listing says the camera does not identify wines or scan labels.

Which wine apps are free?

Most are free to download with a paid tier. Wine Master's listing says it is entirely free, and Hello Vino lists no in-app purchases. Vivino, Wine-Searcher, CellarTracker, Delectable, SomeWine and Winelingo all have a free tier and a paid one.

Which wine app scans restaurant wine lists?

Vivino (listed as a Premium benefit), CellarTracker (listed under Beta in its help pages), SomeWine (Pro) and Winelingo (Premium) all describe wine-list scanning. All four describe pointing you to the bottles on the list that suit your own taste.
