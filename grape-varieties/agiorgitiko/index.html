Why it matters

Agiorgitiko (pronounced ah-yor-YEE-tee-ko) is Greece’s most-planted red grape and its friendliest one. Its tongue-twister name simply means “St. George’s grape,” and it is the heart of red Nemea in the Peloponnese.

Think of it as a Mediterranean crowd-pleaser: deep colour, ripe plummy fruit, soft tannins and a gentle dusting of sweet spice. It rarely bites, which makes it an easy first step into Greek reds.

Its real charm is range. Few grapes can turn out a fresh dry rosé, a juicy everyday red, and a serious oak-aged bottle that ages for a decade — Agiorgitiko does all three from the same vineyards.

Styles to know

Young & unoaked: bright, soft and full of red-plum and cherry fruit. Chill it lightly and treat it like a Mediterranean answer to easy-drinking Merlot.

Barrel-aged Nemea: time in oak adds vanilla, sweet spice and a firmer grip, turning the wine richer and more structured for the table or the cellar.

Rosé: dry, pale and red-fruited — one of Greece’s most reliable pink wines.

Sweet (rare): a few producers make a dried-grape dessert version, intensely raisined and figgy. Worth trying if you spot it.

Where it shines

Nemea is the benchmark. This single appellation grows Agiorgitiko across a big range of altitudes, and that elevation is the key to style.

• Valley floors (warm): richer, riper, softer wines built for early drinking. • High slopes (cool): more acidity, firmer structure and greater ageing potential.

Because one name covers both, two “Nemea” bottles can taste quite different — a clue to read the producer, not just the region.

What shapes it

Oak ageing: resting the wine in barrels softens it and layers in vanilla and spice. Unoaked versions stay fruitier and fresher — the label or price often hints which you’re getting.

Altitude: grapes grown higher up ripen more slowly in cooler air, keeping more acidity and freshness. This is Agiorgitiko’s main quality lever in Nemea.

Acidity management: in the warmest sites the grape can lose its natural tartness, so good growers pick earlier or work cooler hillsides to keep the wine lively rather than flabby.

Good to know

Food: its soft tannins and ripe fruit make it hugely versatile — lamb, tomato-based stews, moussaka, grilled vegetables, even a burger.

Ageing: everyday bottles are best young; serious oak-aged Nemea can improve for 5–10 years, gaining leather and dried-fruit notes.

Myth-buster: the scary name puts people off, but this is one of the gentlest, most approachable reds in the Mediterranean — a soft landing, not a challenge.

Food pairing. Agiorgitiko is wonderfully food-friendly and loves medium-intensity dishes like lamb, tomato-rich stews, and grilled meats. Lean into red cherry, plum, and a hint of sweet spice.
