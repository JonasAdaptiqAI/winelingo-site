The everyday Italian white

Trebbiano is one of the most planted white grapes on the planet — the quiet workhorse behind oceans of crisp, light Italian white wine. It’s not flashy, and that’s rather the point.

In its everyday guise it makes simple, fresh, easy-drinking wine: light-bodied, high in acidity, gently citrusy and built for a sunny afternoon rather than a cellar.

A word of warning: “Trebbiano” is not one grape but a sprawling family of unrelated varieties that happen to share the name. The most widespread is Trebbiano Toscano, which the French call Ugni Blanc — the base for Cognac and Armagnac brandy.

Aromas & flavours: lemon, green apple, white peach, almond and fresh herbs — clean and unobtrusive.

Styles to know

Trebbiano covers more ground than its humble reputation suggests — partly because the name covers several different grapes.

Light & crisp everyday whites: the most common style, usually from high-cropping Trebbiano Toscano. Neutral, zesty, refreshing and inexpensive — the easygoing white in countless Italian trattorias.

Trebbiano d’Abruzzo (the serious side): from central Italy, a handful of dedicated growers (Valentini and Emidio Pepe are legends) coax remarkable depth, texture and longevity from the grape. The twist: their best bottles are largely a different, superior variety — Trebbiano Abruzzese — not the humble Toscano, which is a big part of why they can be so profound.

Distilling base: as Ugni Blanc in France, its high acidity and neutral flavour make it ideal for distilling into Cognac and Armagnac.

Vin Santo and blends: it’s also used in Italy’s sweet, raisiny Vin Santo dessert wine and as a blending partner across central Italy.

Where it shines

Trebbiano is spread across Italy and France under many names — and, confusingly, several of those names belong to genuinely different grapes.

• Abruzzo (central Italy) — home of Trebbiano d’Abruzzo, where the best producers make the most ambitious, age-worthy versions, largely from the distinct Trebbiano Abruzzese.

• Tuscany, Umbria, Lazio and Emilia-Romagna — vast plantings of everyday whites, mostly true Trebbiano Toscano.

• Soave — Trebbiano di Soave plays a small supporting role to Garganega here, though despite the name it’s genetically Verdicchio rather than a true Trebbiano — a neat example of how loosely the name gets used.

• Cognac & Armagnac (France) — as Ugni Blanc, it’s the dominant grape for France’s great brandies, prized for acidity rather than flavour.

The name on the label varies wildly by region, so it often hides in plain sight.

What shapes it

High yields vs. low yields. Left to crop heavily, Trebbiano Toscano gives thin, neutral wine — which is why so much of it is forgettable. Restrict the vine to small crops and the grapes gain real concentration and character.

Which grape, really. Because the name covers several varieties, what’s in the glass depends hugely on the actual grape: humble Trebbiano Toscano makes the everyday stuff, while Trebbiano Abruzzese in skilled hands makes something far deeper. Same word, very different ceilings.

Naturally high acidity. This is the family’s shared trait: it keeps wines fresh and is exactly what brandy-makers want, since acidity carries through distillation.

Winemaking. Most is made in neutral steel tanks (which add no flavour, preserving freshness). A few ambitious producers use skin contact (leaving juice with the grape skins for grip and colour) or lees ageing (resting on spent yeast for a creamy, bready richness) to add texture and complexity.

Good to know

Food: a classic everyday companion — seafood, fried fish, light pasta, salads and antipasti. Its acidity and low profile make it endlessly versatile and never overpowering. The serious Abruzzo bottles can stand up to richer dishes like roast chicken or creamy pasta.

Serving: drink the everyday style cold and young — it’s about freshness, not ageing — while the top Abruzzo wines can take a little more age and a less icy serve.

Value: few wines deliver more easy refreshment for the price. Set your expectations to “crisp and cheerful” and it rarely disappoints — and the rare Abruzzese cult bottles, while not cheap, reward the curiosity.

Common confusion: “Trebbiano” isn’t one grape but a group, and the same vine appears as Ugni Blanc in France. So if you’ve sipped a young Cognac, you’ve already met Trebbiano in spirit form.

Food pairing. Trebbiano is light and food-friendly, perfect for fried seafood, simple pasta, and fresh salads. Its crisp citrus snap brightens with lemon, parsley, and sea salt.

Producers to seek out for Trebbiano

Most Trebbiano is a modest everyday white, but in Abruzzo a handful of growers turn it into one of Italy’s most profound and long-lived whites — that is where to seek it out.

1

Valentini

Abruzzo’s legendary estate, whose Trebbiano d’Abruzzo is one of Italy’s greatest whites.

2

Emidio Pepe

Biodynamic Abruzzo producer of a famously age-worthy Trebbiano d’Abruzzo.

3

Tiberio

Abruzzo estate whose Fonte Canale is a benchmark for Trebbiano Abruzzese.

4

Masciarelli

Respected Abruzzo house with fine Trebbiano d’Abruzzo under its Castello di Semivicoli and Marina Cvetic labels.
