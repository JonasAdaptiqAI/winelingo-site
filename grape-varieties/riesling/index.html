The most misunderstood great white

Many wine experts, if forced to pick one white grape for life, would choose Riesling. It is electrically aromatic, transparent to where it’s grown, and capable of greatness from bone-dry to lusciously sweet. Yet beginners often skip it, fearing it’s “all sweet.”

The truth: most fine Riesling is dry or barely off-dry, and even the sweet ones are kept brilliantly fresh by the grape’s defining feature — searing, mouth-watering acidity. That acid is the backbone that makes everything else sing.

Riesling is also a chameleon of place. Grown in slate, it tastes of slate; grown in a cool, sunny corner, it captures lime and orchard fruit with uncanny purity. It’s almost never oaked, so nothing hides between you and the grape.

Styles to know — and how to read the sweetness

Sweetness is Riesling’s great source of confusion. The label usually tells you, if you know the words.

Dry (Trocken): crisp, zesty, lime-and-green-apple, often racy and light. Most modern German and all Alsace/Australian Riesling leans here. “Trocken” on a German label means dry.

Off-dry (Halbtrocken / Feinherb): a whisper of sweetness that softens the acid — versatile and food-friendly.

Fruity / lightly sweet (Kabinett, Spätlese): German ripeness terms (Prädikat) that signal riper grapes and usually more sweetness — but the high acid keeps them feeling fresh, not sugary. Note that these terms describe grape ripeness, not guaranteed sweetness: an Auslese, for instance, can be vinified bone-dry, off-dry or richly sweet.

Dessert-sweet (Beerenauslese, Trockenbeerenauslese, Eiswein): intensely sweet, honeyed nectar. Beerenauslese and Trockenbeerenauslese come from super-ripe, often nobly-rotted grapes; Eiswein is made from grapes left to freeze on the vine (not necessarily botrytised). Sweeter Auslese bottlings sit just below these. Rare and ageless.

Aromas & flavours: lime, green apple, white peach, apricot, honeysuckle and wet stone — plus, with age, a famous (and prized) whiff of “petrol” or kerosene.

Where it shines

Riesling is a cool-climate specialist, and each region gives a distinct accent.

• Mosel (Germany): the featherweight benchmark — low alcohol, piercing acidity, lime and slate, often delicately off-dry. Steep blue-slate slopes by the river. • Rheingau & Pfalz (Germany): a touch fuller and riper, more orchard fruit, often made dry. • Alsace (France): dry, powerful, structured and built for the table. • Clare & Eden Valley (Australia): bone-dry, intense lime-and-grapefruit, with a crisp “lime cordial” purity. • Finger Lakes (USA), Wachau (Austria), Niagara (Canada): rising cool-climate stars across dry and off-dry styles.

The “petrol” note (technically the compound TDN) is not a fault — it’s a hallmark of mature Riesling and a sign of a wine evolving beautifully.

Good to know

The food superpower: that high acidity and optional touch of sweetness make Riesling the ultimate match for spicy and aromatic food — Thai, Indian, Szechuan, Vietnamese. A lightly off-dry Riesling cools chilli heat the way water can’t.

It ages magnificently: few whites live as long. Fine Riesling can improve for 10–30+ years, trading primary fruit for honey, ginger and that petrol complexity, all on a frame of undimmed acidity.

The aging myth: sweetness does not mean low quality. The world’s most coveted, long-lived white wines include intensely sweet Rieslings; the sugar is balanced, deliberate and protective.

Serving tip: don’t over-chill — too cold and the perfume disappears. Around 8–10°C lets the aromatics open. Screwcaps (common on Riesling) keep it pristine and are a quality signal here, not a cheap one.

Food pairing. Riesling is wonderfully food-friendly and loves bright, aromatic dishes; off-dry styles tame spicy Thai and Indian curries, while dry ones lift trout, pork, and apple. Play up its notes of lime, green apple, and honey.

Producers to seek out for Riesling

Great Riesling runs from bone-dry to honey-sweet, so start in Germany’s Mosel and Nahe, then cross to Alsace for the drier, fuller style.

1

Egon Müller

Saar estate whose Scharzhofberger is among the world’s most coveted Riesling.

2

Joh. Jos. Prüm

Mosel benchmark for delicate, long-lived Riesling from Wehlener Sonnenuhr.

3

Dönnhoff

Nahe estate making precise, mineral Riesling across dry and sweet styles.

4

Trimbach

Alsace house whose Clos Sainte Hune is a reference for dry Riesling.

5

Weingut Keller

Rheinhessen estate renowned for taut, age-worthy dry Riesling.

FAQ

Questions & answers

Is Riesling a red or white wine?

Riesling is a white wine grape. Aromatic white ranging from bone-dry to lusciously sweet.

What food pairs with Riesling?

Riesling is wonderfully food-friendly and loves bright, aromatic dishes; off-dry styles tame spicy Thai and Indian curries, while dry ones lift trout, pork, and apple. Play up its notes of lime, green apple, and honey.

How should I serve Riesling?

Serve at 8–10°C / 46–50°F. Use a universal or aromatic white glass.
