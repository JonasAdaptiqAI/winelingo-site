Why Syrah matters

Syrah is one of the world’s great red grapes — bold, dark and savoury, capable of producing some of the most powerful and age-worthy reds made anywhere. If you like a full-bodied red with a peppery, smoky kick, this is your grape.

Its hallmark is the contrast between rich, dark fruit and a savoury, spicy edge. Expect deep blackberry and blueberry, then a twist of black pepper, smoked meat, olive and violet. Full-bodied, with firm tannin and moderate acidity, it is a wine of weight and depth.

You’ll see two names on labels — Syrah and Shiraz — for the very same grape. The name is a strong hint about style: “Syrah” usually signals a more savoury, restrained, European-influenced wine, while “Shiraz” (Australia’s name for it) usually means a riper, fruitier, more powerful one. Same grape, different intent.

• Aromas & flavours: blackberry, blueberry, plum, black pepper, smoked meat, black olive, violet, liquorice

Styles to know

Syrah’s two faces are well worth learning, because they taste genuinely different.

Cool-climate, savoury Syrah: The Northern Rhône template. Medium-to-full bodied, peppery and meaty, with restrained dark fruit, olive, smoke and a fresh, almost taut structure. Elegant and complex rather than sheerly powerful.

Warm-climate, fruity Shiraz: The Australian template. Full-bodied, ripe and generous, with sweet blackberry and blueberry jam, chocolate, mocha and soft, plush tannin. Higher in alcohol and immediately enjoyable.

Blended Syrah: In the Southern Rhône and across the world it’s a key partner in GSM blends (Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre), where it adds colour, dark fruit and a peppery backbone.

Co-fermented with a little Viognier: A famous Northern Rhône trick (in Côte-Rôtie) where a tiny amount of the white grape Viognier is fermented alongside the Syrah, lifting the aromatics with floral perfume and stabilising the colour.

• Best examples: Hermitage and Côte-Rôtie (Northern Rhône), Barossa Shiraz (Australia)

Where it shines

Syrah travels brilliantly, taking on its surroundings.

• Northern Rhône, France — the ancestral home and the benchmark for savoury Syrah. Hermitage is powerful and age-worthy; Côte-Rôtie is perfumed and elegant; Cornas is dark and brooding; Crozes-Hermitage and Saint-Joseph offer the style at gentler prices. • Barossa Valley, Australia — the icon of rich, ripe, full-throttle Shiraz, with old vines giving extraordinary depth and sweet dark fruit. • Other Australia — the warm, maritime McLaren Vale gives plush, ripe Shiraz, while genuinely cool-climate regions such as Eden Valley (the Barossa’s high ground) and parts of Victoria (Grampians, Beechworth, Yarra Valley) give a more peppery, restrained spectrum. • Washington State & California (USA) — both fruit-driven warmth and increasingly serious, savoury cool-climate styles. • Chile, South Africa, Southern France — excellent value, often peppery and Rhône-leaning.

What shapes it

Several factors push Syrah toward either its savoury or its fruity self.

Climate: The decisive lever. Cooler sites preserve acidity and accentuate the pepper, olive and smoked-meat savour; warmer sites ripen the fruit into rich, jammy, chocolatey flavours and raise the alcohol.

That black pepper: Syrah’s signature peppery note comes from a natural aroma compound called rotundone, which builds up more in cooler climates and cooler vintages — a big reason Northern Rhône and cool-climate Syrah taste so peppery.

Oak: Ageing in oak barrels is common and adds vanilla, smoke, cedar and spice while smoothing the tannin. New American oak (favoured in some Australian styles) gives sweeter, coconut-and-vanilla notes; French oak is subtler.

Co-fermentation with Viognier: Even a few percent of this white grape, fermented together with the Syrah, lifts the perfume with apricot and floral notes and deepens the colour — a hallmark of Côte-Rôtie.

Tannin and structure: Firm tannin and concentrated fruit are what let top Syrah age for decades.

Good to know

At the table: Syrah is a meat-lover’s wine. Its pepper and savour make it superb with grilled and roasted red meats, lamb, game, sausages, barbecue and peppered steak. The smoky styles love anything off the grill; richer Shiraz stands up to sticky barbecue sauce and hard cheese.

Ageing: Top Northern Rhône Syrah and great Barossa Shiraz are among the longest-lived reds, evolving over 10–20 years or more as the fruit gives way to leather, game, tobacco and dried herbs. Everyday versions are made to enjoy young. Decant young, structured examples for an hour.

Value: Côte-du-Rhône, Crozes-Hermitage and Saint-Joseph offer the savoury Rhône style affordably, and Chile and South Africa deliver remarkable peppery Syrah for the money.

Common confusion: Syrah and Shiraz are identical genetically — only the name and usual style differ. And don’t confuse Syrah with Petite Sirah, an unrelated grape (its real name is Durif) that makes even darker, more tannic wines.

Food pairing. Syrah is bold and smoky, perfect for grilled steak, lamb, sausages, and peppered dishes. Bring out its blackberry, black-pepper, and bacon-smoke character with rosemary, olive, and char.

Producers to seek out for Syrah

For Syrah at its most profound, head to the steep hills of the Northern Rhône, above all Hermitage and Côte-Rôtie, where a handful of estates set the world standard.

1

Domaine Jean-Louis Chave

Hermitage’s benchmark house, family growers for centuries

2

Guigal

Côte-Rôtie estate famed for its single-vineyard La Landonne and La Mouline

3

Paul Jaboulet Aîné

Maker of Hermitage La Chapelle, a historic Syrah reference

4

Domaine Jamet

Côte-Rôtie grower prized for fragrant, structured Syrah

5

M. Chapoutier

Biodynamic Hermitage house with a deep Northern Rhône range
