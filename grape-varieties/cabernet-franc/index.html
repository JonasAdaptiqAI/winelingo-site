Cabernet Franc Wine

Cabernet Franc is an aromatic black grape that makes medium-bodied, often herbaceous red wines and is one of the parents of Cabernet Sauvignon.

Look for a leafy, green bell-pepper or pencil-shaving note over bright red fruit, with lighter tannins and higher acidity than its offspring Cabernet Sauvignon. Think of it as a fresher, more floral and herbal cousin of Cabernet Sauvignon, closer in weight to Merlot but more aromatic and savoury. Loire examples (Chinon, Bourgueil) show this most clearly, while Right Bank Bordeaux uses it to add perfume to blends.

Why it matters

Cabernet Franc is the quiet parent behind two superstars. Cross it with Sauvignon Blanc (yes, the white grape) and you get Cabernet Sauvignon — and it’s also a direct parent of Merlot. So even if you’ve never bought a bottle labelled “Cabernet Franc,” you’ve almost certainly tasted its DNA.

On its own it’s a medium-bodied red that trades muscle for fragrance and finesse. Where Cabernet Sauvignon is the broad-shouldered one, Cab Franc is leaner, more aromatic, and quicker to charm.

Its signature is a leafy, peppery lift sitting over red fruit. Beginners sometimes meet it as “that wine that smells a bit green” — in the right hands that herbal streak is a feature, not a fault.

Styles to know

Fresh & unoaked (Loire-style): Bright, crunchy and medium-bodied, built around raspberry and a tell-tale herbal note. Often slightly chilled, sometimes almost like a grown-up’s juicy red. Great everyday drinking.

Structured & age-worthy: Riper, oak-aged versions (warmer Loire vintages, top Right Bank Bordeaux blends, parts of Italy) add darker fruit, more tannin and the ability to improve for a decade or more.

As a blending partner: In Bordeaux’s Right Bank it adds perfume and backbone to Merlot. You rarely see it named, but it’s doing a lot of the aromatic heavy lifting.

Where it shines

• Loire Valley, France — the benchmark. Chinon, Bourgueil and Saumur-Champigny make medium-bodied reds full of raspberry, crushed gravel and that classic leafy lift. Cooler vintages taste greener; warm ones, riper and rounder. • Bordeaux, France (Right Bank) — a key blending grape in Saint-Émilion; Château Cheval Blanc is famously Cab Franc-heavy. • Loire’s sweet side — it even makes delicate rosé and sparkling wines. • Beyond France — Italy (Friuli, Tuscany), and increasingly impressive single-varietal bottlings from California, Argentina, Virginia and Canada.

The through-line: cooler sites accentuate herbs and freshness, warmer sites push toward riper, plummier fruit.

What shapes it

Ripeness is everything. That “green” pyrazine character (the same compound that makes green peppers smell like green peppers) fades as grapes ripen. Underripe = leafy and lean; fully ripe = sweet raspberry and plum. This is why the same grape tastes so different from a cool year versus a hot one.

Oak ageing: Time in oak barrels adds spice, smoke and a rounder texture, and tames the herbal edge. Unoaked versions stay zippier and more fruit-forward.

Whole-bunch / carbonic touches: Some modern Loire producers ferment with whole grape clusters to boost floral perfume and juicy texture — think violets and crushed raspberry.

• Aromas & flavours: raspberry, red plum, violet, bell pepper, dried herbs, pencil shavings • Tell-tale sign: a green, leafy or capsicum note — pronounced when grapes are less ripe, softer when fully ripe

Good to know

Food: Its freshness and herbal side make it brilliant with food — roast chicken, pork, charcuterie, mushroom dishes, herb-crusted lamb. A lighter Loire style takes a light chill and even pairs with salmon.

Serving: Don’t serve it too warm. Around 15-16°C (slightly cool) keeps it lively; over-warm it can taste soupy and over-herbal.

Value: One of the great-value reds. Loire Cab Franc often over-delivers for the price compared to big-name Cabernet Sauvignon.

Common confusion: “Cabernet” on a label usually means Cabernet Sauvignon — Cabernet Franc is the lighter, more aromatic cousin, and almost always says “Franc” in full.

Food pairing. Cabernet Franc is savory and herbal, great with roast chicken, grilled vegetables, and herb-crusted lamb. Lean into raspberry, bell pepper, and graphite freshness.
