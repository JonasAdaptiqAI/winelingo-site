An ancient white with grip

Greco is one of southern Italy’s oldest and most distinctive white grapes, grown mainly in the volcanic hills of Campania, around Naples. Its name points to Greek associations — Greek settlers were active in southern Italy in antiquity — though the grape’s actual origins are debated, and ‘Greco’ is a catch-all name covering several unrelated varieties. Whatever its history, it has been making serious wine in the region for a very long time.

What sets Greco apart from most Italian whites is its firmness and structure. It’s a medium-bodied wine with real grip and a savoury, mineral edge, more about texture and tension than easy fruitiness.

Aromas & flavours: peach, lemon, pear, green almond, a smoky or flinty minerality, and a faintly waxy, savoury depth.

How it differs from Fiano

Greco’s natural rival is Fiano, Campania’s other great white — and tasting them side by side is the best way to learn both.

Fiano is softer, rounder and nuttier, with honey, hazelnut and a gentler feel.

Greco is firmer, more mineral and more savoury, with brighter acidity, a smoky-stony edge and a tighter, grippier texture. It’s the more structured and tightly wound of the two.

Think of Fiano as the rounder, more immediately charming wine, and Greco as the leaner, more serious one that demands a little patience — and often rewards it with food.

Where it shines

Greco is firmly a southern Italian specialty, at its best on the volcanic soils of inland Campania.

• Greco di Tufo DOCG — the benchmark, from the village of Tufo and its sulphur-rich volcanic soils, which give the wine its smoky, mineral signature; the finest examples can age for years • Wider Campania — used in blends and varietal wines across the region • Calabria & elsewhere in the south — also planted, sometimes under local names

Note that ‘Greco’ is a confusingly broad term in Italy: several unrelated grapes carry the name, but Greco di Tufo is the one that matters most.

What shapes it

Volcanic soil: the sulphurous, tuff (volcanic rock) soils of the Tufo area are central to Greco’s character, giving that flinty, smoky minerality you rarely find elsewhere.

Acidity and structure: Greco naturally holds good acidity and an unusual phenolic grip for a white — it can feel almost gently tannic, partly thanks to its skins — which is why the wines feel firm and can age.

Lees ageing: producers often rest the wine on its lees (spent yeast cells) to build texture and a savoury, leesy richness that complements the mineral backbone.

Oak: mostly avoided. Greco’s appeal is its mineral, savoury freshness, so the great majority is made in steel to preserve that bright, stony character.

Good to know

A food wine first. Greco’s acidity, grip and saline edge make it a natural with seafood — grilled fish, shellfish, salt cod — as well as fried vegetables and creamy pasta. It’s built for the table rather than for sipping alone.

Give it air and a little warmth. Served too cold, Greco can taste austere and closed; around 10–12°C lets its peach, smoke and texture show. A young, tightly wound bottle can even benefit from a short time open.

Aging: unusually for a southern white, good Greco di Tufo can develop for 5–8 years, gaining honeyed, petrol-like and smoky complexity.

Don’t confuse the name: ‘Greco’ appears on several unrelated Italian grapes — for the real thing, look for Greco di Tufo.

Food pairing. Crisp and savory from southern Italy, it pairs with shellfish, grilled vegetables, and pasta in light sauces. Its peach, almond, and citrus-zest accents love a drizzle of good olive oil.

Producers to seek out for Greco

Greco is at its finest as Greco di Tufo, the sulphur-rich hills of Campania’s Irpinia, where a handful of estates make firm, mineral whites that reward a few years.

1

Mastroberardino

Irpinia house credited with saving Greco; the Novaserra cru.

2

Feudi di San Gregorio

Single-vineyard Cutizzi Greco di Tufo from Sorbo Serpico.

3

Benito Ferrara

Celebrated for the elegant Greco di Tufo Vigna Cicogna.

4

Pietracupa

Montefredane estate making mineral, ageworthy Greco.
