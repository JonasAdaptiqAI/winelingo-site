Spain’s quiet star white

Godello is one of the great comeback stories in wine. In the rugged hills of northwest Spain it was almost extinct by the 1970s, down to a few hundred vines, before growers rescued it. Today it’s hailed as one of Spain’s finest white grapes — and many people meet it without realising how good it is.

It sits in a lovely middle ground: more textured and rounded than crisp, light whites, but fresher and more mineral than rich, oaky ones. Picture a wine with the body of a white Burgundy and the cut of a coastal Spanish white.

Aromas & flavours: ripe pear, apple, white peach, hazelnut, fennel, and a stony, saline minerality.

How it compares to Albariño

Godello is constantly compared to Albariño, Spain’s better-known northwestern white — and the contrast is the quickest way to understand it.

Albariño is zesty, aromatic and citrus-driven, all about freshness and zing.

Godello is fuller, rounder and more textural, with stone fruit, a nutty depth and a broader, more mouth-filling feel. It trades some of Albariño’s sharp aromatic snap for body and structure.

If Albariño is the bright, breezy seafood white, Godello is the one you reach for when you want something with a bit more weight and seriousness — yet it still keeps a refreshing mineral backbone.

Where it shines

Godello’s home is the green, hilly, Atlantic-influenced corner of northwest Spain, where steep slate and granite slopes give the wines their mineral tension.

• Valdeorras (Galicia) — the grape’s heartland and benchmark, on prized slate soils; the source of its best, most ageworthy wines • Bierzo (Castilla y León) — just east, making rounder, fruit-forward styles • Ribeira Sacra & Monterrei — dramatic, steep-sloped Galician zones producing fresh, mineral examples

It’s also grown across the border in Portugal, where it’s known as Gouveio and used in white Douro and Vinho Verde blends.

What shapes it

Godello is unusually responsive to how it’s made, which is why styles vary so much.

Stainless steel: the freshest, most mineral, citrus-and-orchard-fruit style — clean and tank-fermented.

Lees ageing: many of the best wines rest on their lees (the spent yeast left after fermentation), often with bâtonnage — stirring those lees through the wine. This adds a creamy texture and savoury, bready, nutty depth without any oak flavour.

Oak: top producers age Godello in barrels to make richer, more complex, Burgundy-like whites. Used with restraint it adds spice and length; overdone, it can mask the grape’s mineral charm.

Soil: slate and granite are key to that flinty, saline edge.

Good to know

Great value, for now. Godello still flies under the radar compared with white Burgundy or even Albariño, so quality-to-price is excellent — though prices are rising as word spreads.

Food: its texture makes it a fantastic table wine — grilled fish, roast chicken, pork, creamy or buttery sauces, hard cheeses. It handles richer dishes that would overwhelm a lighter white.

Ageing: unusually for a Spanish white, the best Godello rewards a few years in bottle, deepening into honey, nuts and a waxy richness. Steel-only styles are best drunk young.

Serve around 10–12°C — too cold and you’ll mute its lovely texture and stone-fruit depth.

Food pairing. Textured and mineral, this Spanish white suits grilled fish, roast chicken, and creamy risottos. Highlight its stone-fruit, citrus, and saline notes with herbs and a squeeze of lemon.
