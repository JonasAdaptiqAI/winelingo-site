Italy’s great-value everyday red

Montepulciano is the workhorse red of Italy’s Adriatic coast — above all Abruzzo — and one of the best-value bottles in any wine shop. Deeply coloured, medium-to-full-bodied and packed with juicy dark fruit, it offers softness and generosity at a friendly price.

Think ripe black cherry and plum, a rustic herbal-earthy edge, and gentle, smooth tannins. It is uncomplicated, warming and made for the table.

Why it matters: few grapes deliver this much honest pleasure for the money. Montepulciano d’Abruzzo is the reliable, crowd-pleasing red that overdelivers on a weeknight.

The name trap: clear this up first

This is the single most confusing name in Italian wine, so it is worth nailing down.

Montepulciano (the grape): the red variety described here, grown mainly in Abruzzo on the Adriatic coast. The wine is labelled Montepulciano d’Abruzzo.

Vino Nobile di Montepulciano (the wine): a completely different, more prestigious wine from the Tuscan hill town called Montepulciano — and it is made mainly from the Sangiovese grape (locally called Prugnolo Gentile), not from Montepulciano at all.

The rule of thumb: “Montepulciano d’Abruzzo” = the Montepulciano grape. “…di Montepulciano” (from Montepulciano) = the Tuscan place, so it is Sangiovese. Same word, two totally different wines.

Styles to know, and where it shines

Easy, everyday Montepulciano d’Abruzzo: soft, juicy and smooth, with bright black cherry and plum — the classic affordable pizza-and-pasta red, often best with a slight chill.

Riserva and top-estate wines: more concentrated and structured, with firmer tannin and the ability to age, showing dried herbs, liquorice, leather and earth. Proof the grape can be serious and long-lived.

Colline Teramane DOCG: Abruzzo’s top zone, the source of the most structured, age-worthy examples.

Cerasuolo d’Abruzzo: a deeply coloured cherry-pink rosé from the same grape (cerasuolo means “cherry-coloured”) — fuller and more characterful than most rosés, and great with food.

Best examples: Valentini, Emidio Pepe, Tiberio, Cataldi Madonna.

Good to know

Aromas & flavours: black cherry, plum, blackberry, dried herbs, liquorice, and an earthy, savoury undertone.

Food: built for Italian cooking. Tomato-based pasta, pizza, grilled meats, sausages, aubergine dishes and hard cheeses. The soft tannins and dark fruit make it forgiving and adaptable.

Serving: mid-priced examples shine with a slight chill on a warm day; the soft, fruity profile takes well to being served a touch cooler than room temperature.

The value angle: because the basic wines are so cheap and so reliable, Montepulciano is a brilliant house red — and the named single estates show how far the grape can climb.

Food pairing. Montepulciano is hearty and crowd-pleasing, a natural match for pizza, pasta with red sauce, and grilled sausages. Lean into tomato, oregano, and dark-berry flavours to make it pop.
