Tokaj Wine Region

Hungary’s historic and most legendary region, world-renowned for Tokaji Aszú, a golden, honeyed sweet wine, and for increasingly prized dry whites, primarily from Furmint.

Sweet Tokaji Aszú is golden and honeyed, with dried apricot, orange marmalade, quince and spice, yet kept lively by racing acidity so it never feels cloying. Dry Furmint is crisp and mineral, with green apple, citrus and a stony, smoky edge.

Five things to know about Tokaj

  1. Tokaj was classified early, but not first. Vineyard ranking began here in 1730 and a royal decree closed the production district in 1737, which is often called the world’s earliest appellation. Chianti had its boundaries drawn in 1716.
  2. Puttonyos was a recipe, not a rating. A puttony was a hod holding roughly 25 kilograms of shrivelled aszú berries, kneaded to a paste and stirred into a 136-litre Gönc barrel of base wine. Six puttonyos meant six baskets went in.
  3. Some Tokaj is Slovak. About 908 hectares of the historic region lie north of the border, and in February 2013 the EU Court of Justice confirmed that Slovak growers may use the Tokaj name. The vineyard never stopped at the frontier.
  4. Six grapes are permitted and most drinkers can name one. Furmint and Hárslevelű do the heavy lifting, joined by Sárga Muskotály, Zéta, Kövérszőlő and Kabar. Furmint’s acidity is what keeps the sweet wines from collapsing into syrup.
  5. Eszencia barely ferments. The free-run nectar of the shrivelled berries carries 500 to 700 grams of sugar per litre, so much that yeast can hardly work in it. The alcohol rarely climbs past 5 to 6%, and it can take years to get there.

How Tokaj wine tastes

Tokaj is most famous for one of the world’s great sweet wines, Tokaji Aszú. It is deep gold in the glass and gloriously honeyed (think dried apricot, orange marmalade, quince and warm spice), but the thing that makes it special is its acidity: a bright, zesty freshness runs right through the sweetness, so the wine feels lively and lifted rather than thick or cloying.

Increasingly, Tokaj also makes excellent dry white wine, mostly from the Furmint grape. Dry Furmint is crisp and mineral, with green apple, pear and citrus, sometimes a smoky, stony edge, and enough structure to age.

Whether sweet or dry, the common thread is that racy acidity. It is the signature of the region.

Signature styles & what to look for

The legendary style is Tokaji Aszú, made from grapes shrivelled by noble rot (see below). Its sweetness was traditionally measured in puttonyos, and you’ll still see, for example, ‘5 puttonyos’ or ‘6 puttonyos’ on a label, with the higher number meaning a sweeter, more concentrated wine; today most Aszú is made at these top levels. Rarer and sweeter still is Eszencia, the intensely concentrated free-run nectar of the shrivelled grapes.

At the other end, dry Furmint (sometimes labelled ‘Száraz’) is the region’s fast-rising star and a brilliant food wine. Late Harvest and Szamorodni sit in between, offering gently sweet styles.

If you’re buying, the grape and the sweetness level are your main guides, and single-vineyard (‘dűlő’) bottlings from the best sites are where to look for extra character.

The grapes & the land

The heart of Tokaj is Furmint, high in natural acidity and the grape behind both the great sweet wines and the new wave of dry whites, supported by the softer, more floral Hárslevelű and the aromatic Sárgamuskotály (Yellow Muscat).

The region sits where two rivers, the Bodrog and the Tisza, meet, and that is no accident. Their moisture brings autumn mists that encourage noble rot (Botrytis cinerea), a friendly mould that pierces the grape skins and lets water evaporate, shrivelling the berries and concentrating their sugar, acid and flavour. The soils are largely volcanic with patches of loess, and together with Furmint’s naturally firm acidity they keep even the sweetest wines balanced and fresh. It is a combination found in very few places on earth.

Producers to know in Tokaj

1

Royal Tokaji

Influential estate (co-founded with wine writer Hugh Johnson) central to the region’s modern revival, known for single-vineyard Aszú.

2

Disznókő

Leading estate making finely balanced 5- and 6-puttonyos Aszú alongside dry Furmint.

3

Oremus

Estate owned by Spain’s Vega Sicilia, respected for its Aszú and its Mandolás dry Furmint.

4

Hétszőlő

Historic first-growth vineyard estate producing Aszú and dry whites.

5

Szepsy

István Szepsy’s benchmark family winery, a pioneer of single-vineyard dry Furmint and traditional Aszú.

Food pairing. Sweet Aszú with blue cheese, foie gras, and fruit or custard puddings; dry Furmint with roast chicken, pork and richer fish.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Tokaji wine taste?

The famous sweet Tokaji Aszú is golden and honeyed, with dried apricot, marmalade and spice, but lifted by bright acidity so it stays fresh rather than cloying. The region’s dry Furmint is crisp and mineral, with green apple, citrus and a stony edge.

What is Tokaji Aszú and what does puttonyos mean?

Tokaji Aszú is a sweet wine made from grapes shrivelled by noble rot, which concentrates their sugar and flavour. Puttonyos is the traditional measure of sweetness on the label; a higher number, such as 5 or 6 puttonyos, means a sweeter, more concentrated wine.

Which wineries is Tokaj known for?

Royal Tokaji, Disznókő, Oremus and Hétszőlő are among the estates that led the region’s revival for sweet Aszú. Szepsy is a benchmark name, especially for pioneering single-vineyard dry Furmint.
