Kamptal Wine Region

A flagship Austrian white-wine region along the Kamp River, north of the Danube, celebrated for crisp Grüner Veltliner and racy, age-worthy Riesling from steep terraced vineyards.

Country
Austria
World
Old World
Climate

Key grapes
Grüner Veltliner, Riesling, Zweigelt, Pinot Noir

Bone-dry whites with real energy: peppery, grassy Grüner Veltliner with a squeeze of citrus and a savoury, mineral finish, and clean, stony Riesling that runs from lime and white peach to something almost salty. Medium-bodied, low in oak and high in mouth-watering acidity.

How Kamptal wine tastes

Kamptal is white-wine country, and its two stars are Grüner Veltliner and Riesling. Grüner is the everyday hero here: think green apple and lime, a leafy, almost herbal freshness, and a signature twist of white pepper on the finish. It usually sees little or no oak, so what you taste is the fruit and a cool, savoury minerality rather than vanilla or spice from a barrel.

Riesling is the other great grape, and Kamptal makes it bone-dry rather than sweet. Expect bright lime, white peach and a flinty, stony streak, all carried by high acidity that keeps the wine feeling taut and refreshing.

The thread running through both is precision. These are not soft, fruity crowd-pleasers so much as clear, focused wines with a saline, mineral cut. The best examples also age beautifully, softening and deepening into honeyed, toasty flavours over ten years or more.

Signature styles & what to look for

Most Kamptal wine is dry white, split between fresh, food-friendly Grüner Veltliner and steelier Riesling. Some light reds (Zweigelt, Pinot Noir) and a little Chardonnay are made too, but the region’s reputation rests on its whites.

Since 2008 the region has used the Kamptal DAC label, a quality mark that guarantees the wine is a typical dry Grüner Veltliner or Riesling from the area. You will see two main tiers: a lighter, fresher Klassik style meant for early drinking, and a fuller, more concentrated Reserve, which is riper, more powerful and built to age.

Also worth knowing is the word Ried on a label, which means the wine comes from a single named vineyard. The most celebrated is Heiligenstein, a steep, sun-trapping slope prized for Riesling. A Ried wine is usually a producer’s more serious, site-specific bottling.

The grapes & the land

Kamptal follows the Kamp River just north of the Danube in Lower Austria. The climate is cool and continental, and that is the secret to its style: warm, dry air drifting up from the Hungarian plain ripens the grapes, while cold night air sinking off the surrounding forests locks in that bright, nervy acidity.

The soils do the rest. Deep, powdery loess (a fine, wind-blown soil) on the gentler terraces suits Grüner Veltliner, giving it body and spice. On the steep, rocky slopes, primary rock such as the weathered stone of Heiligenstein gives Riesling its flinty, mineral edge and its ability to age. That combination of cool nights and stony ground is why these wines taste so precise rather than soft or heavy.

Producers to know in Kamptal

1

Weingut Bründlmayer

Leading Langenlois family estate known for single-vineyard Grüner Veltliner and Riesling from sites such as Ried Lamm and Heiligenstein.

2

Schloss Gobelsburg

The oldest estate in the Kamptal, with winegrowing traced to Cistercian monks in 1171, known for Heiligenstein Riesling and Ried Lamm Grüner Veltliner.

3

Weingut Loimer

Langenlois estate farmed biodynamically, known for precise, terroir-focused Grüner Veltliner and Riesling.

4

Weingut Hirsch

Biodynamic producer known for pure, dry Rieslings from the Heiligenstein and Gaisberg slopes.

5

Weingut Jurtschitsch

Historic family estate in Langenlois farming organically, respected for its dry Grüner Veltliner and Riesling.

Food pairing. A natural match for lighter dishes: schnitzel and lemon, roast chicken, fresh salads, sushi and green vegetables like asparagus that trip up many other wines. The peppery Grüner is especially handy at the table.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Kamptal wine taste?

Mostly crisp, bone-dry whites. Grüner Veltliner is fresh and peppery with green-apple and citrus notes, while Riesling is stony and taut with lime and white peach. Both are high in refreshing acidity and low in oak.

Which wineries is Kamptal known for?

Well-established names include Bründlmayer, Schloss Gobelsburg, Loimer, Hirsch and Jurtschitsch, all known for dry Grüner Veltliner and Riesling.

Is Kamptal Riesling sweet?

No. Unlike some German Riesling, Kamptal Riesling is made bone-dry, with the fruit balanced by high acidity and a mineral, stony character.
