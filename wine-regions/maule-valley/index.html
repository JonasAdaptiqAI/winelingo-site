Maule Valley Wine

Chile’s largest and one of its oldest wine valleys, increasingly prized for old, un-irrigated bush vines (low, free-standing vines without a trellis).

Country
Chile
World
New World
Climate
Cool, rain-influenced Mediterranean
Key grapes
Carignan, País, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot

Traditionally rustic, juicy Chilean reds with dark plum and blackberry fruit, earthy, spicy notes and firm structure. Old-vine Carignan in particular is deep-coloured and savoury with bright acidity and a wild, herby edge.

How Maule Valley wine tastes

Maule wears two hats. The classic, everyday style is generous, easy-drinking Chilean red, soft plum and blackberry fruit with a warm, sunny ripeness. But the wines that have made Maule exciting again come from its old, un-irrigated bush vines.

From those ancient vines, especially Carignan, the wines are altogether more characterful: deeply coloured, savoury and spicy, with dark berry fruit wrapped around firm tannins and a fresh, mouth-watering acidity. There is often an earthy, herby, almost wild note that speaks of the old vineyards.

The País grape, one of Chile’s oldest, gives lighter, juicy, rustic reds that are increasingly bottled on their own as a piece of living history rather than blended away.

Signature styles & what to look for

The name to know is old-vine Carignan. Look for wines carrying the VIGNO seal (short for Vignadores de Carignan): a growers’ association whose rules require dry-farmed vines at least 30 years old, a high proportion of Carignan, and a minimum period of ageing before release. It is Chile’s most meaningful old-vine badge.

Beyond Carignan, seek out characterful bottlings of País, the historic grape once destined only for jug wine, now made into fresh, gluggable and sometimes serious reds. Both grapes are often farmed as free-standing bush vines without a trellis.

Maule also produces plenty of sound, affordable Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Carmenere in a fruitier, more straightforward style, so the region spans humble weeknight reds and genuinely distinctive old-vine wines.

The grapes & the land

Carignan and País are the soul of Maule, alongside the international grapes Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Carmenere. Many of the oldest vines are own-rooted survivors, some over a century old.

Maule sits in Chile’s Central Valley but is one of the country’s coolest, wettest and most humid wine zones, far rainier than the drier regions to the north. That damper Mediterranean climate gives a long, gentle growing season.

That long season is exactly why the reds keep their savoury complexity and fresh acidity rather than tasting merely sunbaked. Dry-farmed on granite and clay soils, the ancient low-yielding vines produce small crops of intensely flavoured fruit, which is where Carignan gets its juicy, structured intensity.

Producers to know in Maule Valley

1

Garage Wine Co.

Small-batch specialist that helped spark the old-vine revival, working with dry-farmed Maule Carignan and País.

2

De Martino

Isla de Maipo family winery and VIGNO founding member, influential in reviving Maule’s old-vine Carignan.

3

Bouchon

French-founded family estate established in 1887, based in Maule and known for old-vine País and Carignan.

4

Gillmore

Boutique Maule estate championing old-vine, dry-farmed reds including Carignan.

Food pairing. Great with grilled and slow-cooked meats, Chilean-style stews, chorizo and empanadas; the old-vine Carignan’s acidity and spice suit hearty, rustic cooking.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Maule Valley wine taste?

Everyday Maule reds are soft, juicy and fruity, but the region’s prized old-vine Carignan is deep, savoury and spicy, with dark berry fruit, firm tannins and bright, fresh acidity. País gives lighter, rustic, gluggable reds.

Which wineries is Maule Valley known for?

Notable Maule producers include Garage Wine Co., De Martino, Bouchon and Gillmore. Several are members of VIGNO, the association dedicated to reviving Maule’s old-vine Carignan.

What is VIGNO?

VIGNO (Vignadores de Carignan) is a growers’ association for Maule old-vine Carignan. Wines carrying its seal must come from dry-farmed vines at least 30 years old and be aged for a set period before release.
