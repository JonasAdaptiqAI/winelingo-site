Friuli-Venezia Giulia Wine

A northeastern corner of Italy bordering Slovenia and Austria, regarded as the country’s benchmark region for aromatic, textural white wines and a pioneer of amber ‘orange’ wines.

Country
Italy
World
Old World
Climate
Cool continental with Alpine and Adriatic influence
Key grapes
Friulano, Pinot Grigio, Ribolla Gialla, Sauvignon Blanc

Friuli’s whites are crisp, textural and gently aromatic, with flavours of pear, white peach, almond and fresh herbs over bright acidity and a savoury, saline, faintly mineral finish. Its famous amber ‘orange’ wines are deeper coloured, grippy and tea-like, tasting of dried apricot, walnut and orange peel.

How Friuli-Venezia Giulia wine tastes

The classic Friulian white is dry, fresh and beautifully textured. Rather than being loud or perfumed, it tends towards subtle aromas of pear, white peach, apple and almond, with fresh herbal and floral notes and a distinctive savoury, saline ‘cut’ on the finish that locals link to the region’s stony soils.

Friulano, the signature grape, adds a gentle bitter-almond twist. Ribolla Gialla is zesty and citrussy, Sauvignon Blanc here is more restrained and stony than in New Zealand, and Pinot Grigio is taken more seriously than almost anywhere, made dry, mineral and food-friendly.

Then there are the amber (orange) wines. These are white grapes fermented on their skins for weeks, the way reds are made. The result is an amber-gold wine with real grip and a tannic, tea-like texture, tasting of dried apricot, walnut, orange peel and honey. They are savoury and structured rather than sweet.

Reds are a smaller story but rewarding: the local Refosco gives juicy, dark-fruited, slightly herbal wines.

Signature styles & what to look for

Two hillside zones make the most celebrated wines: Collio and Colli Orientali del Friuli (now often labelled Friuli Colli Orientali). Both are DOC appellations, and seeing them on a label is a good sign of hillside, quality-focused wine. The flatter Friuli Grave and Friuli Isonzo areas make more everyday bottles.

For the textural amber style, look for terms like ’orange wine’, ‘skin-contact’, ‘macerato’ or ’ramato’ (a coppery, lightly skin-contact Pinot Grigio), and for wines from Oslavia, the village at the heart of the movement.

The region also has a couple of prized sweet wines under DOCG rules: Picolit and Ramandolo, both rare, honeyed dessert wines. As a rough guide, the more specific the hillside name on the label, the more serious the wine.

The grapes & the land

Tucked into Italy’s north-eastern corner against Slovenia and the Alps, Friuli gets cool Alpine air from the north and milder breezes off the Adriatic to the south. That meeting of influences gives a long, gentle ripening season that locks in freshness and delicate aromatics.

The best vineyards sit on hillsides of a marl-and-sandstone soil the locals call ponca, which is where the wines get their savoury, mineral, saline character. Key grapes are the native Friulano and Ribolla Gialla, alongside Pinot Grigio, Sauvignon Blanc and the red Refosco.

The region is also the birthplace of Italy’s modern orange-wine revival, led by growers who buried Georgian-style clay vessels and returned to long skin-fermentation for their whites.

Food pairing. Fresh Friulian whites are lovely with prosciutto (the famous San Daniele ham is local), seafood, risotto and the region’s Alpine cheeses. The grippier amber wines have enough structure for richer, spiced and slow-cooked dishes, cured meats and hard cheeses.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Friuli-Venezia Giulia wine taste?

The whites are crisp, textured and gently aromatic, with pear, almond, herbs and a savoury saline finish. The amber ‘orange’ wines are deeper, grippier and tea-like, tasting of dried apricot, walnut and orange peel.

Which wineries is Friuli-Venezia Giulia known for?

Livio Felluga, Jermann and Schiopetto for classic whites, and Gravner and Radikon of Oslavia for the pioneering amber (orange) wines.

What is an orange wine?

It’s a white wine made like a red: the juice is left to ferment on the grape skins for weeks instead of being pressed off straight away. That gives an amber colour, a grippy tea-like texture and savoury dried-fruit flavours. Friuli is one of its spiritual homes.
