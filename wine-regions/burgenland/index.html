Burgenland Wine Region

Austria’s premier red-wine and sweet-wine region, hugging the Hungarian border around shallow Lake Neusiedl, known for juicy, peppery Blaufränkisch and world-class sweet dessert wines.

Key grapes
Blaufränkisch, Zweigelt, St. Laurent, Welschriesling

Reds are juicy and medium to full-bodied, with dark cherry and blackberry fruit, a peppery, spicy edge and fresh acidity. The region’s sweet wines are lusciously honeyed yet balanced, tasting of apricot, dried fruit and marmalade.

Five things to know about Burgenland

  1. The bottle that broke Austrian wine came from Rust. On 27 June 1985 chemists in Stuttgart found diethylene glycol in a 1983 Ruster Auslese. Exports collapsed from about 45 million litres to 4.4 million within a year. Austria’s answer was a wine law now reckoned the world’s strictest.
  2. Rust bought its freedom with wine. On 3 December 1681 the town paid Emperor Leopold I 60,000 gulden and 500 Eimer of Ausbruch, about 30,000 litres, to become a royal free city. Sweet wine literally bought a charter.
  3. The lake behind the sweet wine is a metre deep. Neusiedl spreads over 320 square kilometres with an average depth of about one metre and no natural outflow. It last dried out completely between 1865 and 1871. Its autumn mists are what bring the noble rot.
  4. Blaufränkisch answers to three names. It is Kékfrankos in Hungary and Lemberger in Germany. Crossed with St. Laurent by Fritz Zweigelt in 1922, it also fathered Zweigelt, now the most planted red grape in Austria.
  5. Six appellations, and the first is only from 2005. Mittelburgenland came first, then Leithaberg, Eisenberg and Neusiedlersee, with Rosalia in 2018 and Ruster Ausbruch in 2020. A DAC on the label promises a style, not just a place.

How Burgenland wine tastes

Burgenland is really two pleasures in one region. Its reds, led by Blaufränkisch, taste of dark cherry, blackberry and plum with a signature twist of black pepper and spice, firm but not harsh tannins, and a refreshing streak of acidity that keeps them lively.

Zweigelt gives softer, rounder, cherry-fruited reds for easy drinking, while St. Laurent leans towards a silkier, more Pinot-like style. Across the board these are wines with energy and freshness rather than heaviness.

Then there are the sweet wines, among the finest on earth. Made from grapes shrivelled by noble rot, they are richly honeyed and unctuous, full of apricot, marmalade and dried fruit, yet lifted by enough acidity to stop them cloying.

Signature styles & what to look for

The flagship dry red is Blaufränkisch, peppery and structured, at its best in named zones such as Leithaberg, Mittelburgenland (nicknamed ‘Blaufränkischland’) and Eisenberg, each carrying its own DAC (a protected regional label guaranteeing a typical style). Zweigelt and St. Laurent round out the reds, and popular Zweigelt-based blends are common.

For sweet wines, the villages around Lake Neusiedl are the heartland. Look for Trockenbeerenauslese (TBA), made from individually shrivelled, botrytis-affected berries, and the historic Ruster Ausbruch from the town of Rust. These often use Welschriesling, Chardonnay and other varieties.

If you are new to the region, a village-level Blaufränkisch is a great, food-friendly place to start.

The grapes & the land

The key red grapes are Blaufränkisch (peppery and structured), Zweigelt (juicy and approachable) and St. Laurent (silky and perfumed). Sweet wines draw on Welschriesling, Chardonnay and other white grapes, while Grüner Veltliner adds dry whites.

Burgenland hugs the Hungarian border in Austria’s warm east, and the shallow, wide Lake Neusiedl is the region’s secret. Its water stores heat and moderates the climate, helping the reds ripen fully to their spicy, full-bodied character.

That same lake raises autumn humidity and morning mists, the perfect conditions for Botrytis cinerea, or noble rot, a beneficial mould that shrivels grapes on the vine and concentrates their sugars. This natural gift is what makes the region’s honeyed dessert wines possible.

Producers to know in Burgenland

1

Kracher

World-renowned sweet-wine specialist by Lake Neusiedl, famed for complex botrytised dessert wines.

2

Moric

Roland Velich’s Blaufränkisch-focused estate, celebrated for elegant, old-vine, terroir-driven reds.

3

Umathum

Long-established Neusiedlersee producer known for refined Blaufränkisch, Zweigelt and St. Laurent.

4

Prieler

Leithaberg estate at Schützen making highly regarded Blaufränkisch from limestone-influenced soils.

5

Hans & Anita Nittnaus

Gols-based producer recognised for characterful, terroir-expressive Blaufränkisch and blends.

Food pairing. Peppery Blaufränkisch is superb with roast pork, game, goulash and grilled meats, while soft Zweigelt suits everyday fare. The sweet wines are a classic match for blue cheese, foie gras and fruit-based desserts.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Burgenland wine taste?

The reds are juicy and medium to full-bodied, with dark cherry and blackberry fruit, a peppery, spicy edge and fresh acidity. The sweet wines are richly honeyed with apricot and dried-fruit flavours, balanced by good acidity.

Which wineries is Burgenland known for?

Notable names include Kracher for sweet wines and Moric, Umathum, Prieler and Hans & Anita Nittnaus for Blaufränkisch and other reds.

Why is Burgenland famous for sweet wine?

Humidity and autumn mists from shallow Lake Neusiedl encourage noble rot, a mould that shrivels grapes and concentrates their sugars, giving the region ideal conditions for luscious dessert wines.
