A premium region in Western Australia famed for elegant, structured Cabernet Sauvignon and crisp Chardonnay, plus refreshing Sauvignon Blanc-Semillon blends.

How Margaret River wine tastes

Margaret River is Western Australia’s premium region, and it is best known for a very particular kind of polish. Its Cabernet Sauvignon is elegant and structured rather than big and jammy, with pure blackcurrant (cassis) and dark cherry fruit, a savoury edge and fine, gravelly tannins (the drying grip that lets a red age well).

Its Chardonnay is just as celebrated: ripe stone fruit and citrus framed by a subtle, nutty oak and a bright line of acidity, striking a balance between richness and freshness.

The region’s third signature is a crisp white blend of Sauvignon Blanc and Semillon, zesty and herbal, with grassy, citrus and passionfruit notes. Across all three, the hallmark is balance: ripe, sunny fruit reined in by freshness and structure, giving a refined, almost European sense of restraint on New World grapes.

Signature styles & what to look for

Look first to the two heavyweights: Cabernet Sauvignon (often blended with a little Merlot in the Bordeaux style) and Chardonnay. These are the wines that made the region’s name, and the best are serious, age-worthy bottles.

The other classic is the Sauvignon Blanc-Semillon blend, usually labelled SBS or SSB, a fresh, unoaked white that is one of Australia’s most reliable summer whites. You will also find good Syrah (Shiraz) and some Chenin Blanc.

Australia has no official ageing-tier system, so quality here is signalled by the producer and by the sub-region. Names like Wilyabrup and Wallcliffe on a label point to well-regarded pockets within Margaret River, and many estates put their most ambitious wine into a named reserve or flagship bottling rather than relying on a legal classification.

The grapes & the land

Margaret River juts out into the ocean at the south-western tip of Australia, surrounded by sea on three sides. That gives it a mild, even maritime climate with no real extremes of heat, so the grapes ripen slowly and completely, building flavour while holding on to acidity. This is the key to the region’s balanced, refined style.

The grapes are the classics of France’s Bordeaux: Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot for the reds, Sauvignon Blanc and Semillon for the crisp whites, with Chardonnay from Burgundy completing the set. The moderate climate suits them perfectly, which is why the region is often described as one of the best places outside France to grow Cabernet.

Gravelly, free-draining soils, especially in the prized Wilyabrup area, give the reds their firm structure and gravelly grip, echoing the gravel terraces of Bordeaux itself.

Producers to know in Margaret River

1

Vasse Felix

The region’s founding vineyard, planted in 1967, known for its Cabernet Sauvignon and Chardonnay.

2

Moss Wood

Founding Wilyabrup estate widely regarded as one of Australia’s benchmark Cabernet Sauvignon producers.

3

Cullen Wines

Biodynamic pioneer known for its Diana Madeline Cabernet-Merlot and Chardonnay.

4

Leeuwin Estate

Known for its Art Series Chardonnay, a benchmark Australian Chardonnay, as well as Cabernet Sauvignon.

5

Cape Mentelle

One of the region’s founding estates, known for structured Cabernet Sauvignon.

Food pairing. Cabernet loves roast lamb and grilled beef, while the refined Chardonnay suits roast chicken, pork and richer fish. The crisp Sauvignon-Semillon blend is a natural with fresh seafood, salads and goat’s cheese.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Margaret River wine taste?

Elegant and balanced. Cabernet Sauvignon shows cassis and dark cherry with fine, gravelly tannins; Chardonnay balances stone fruit and citrus with subtle oak; and the Sauvignon-Semillon blends are crisp and herbal.

Which wineries is Margaret River known for?

Well-established names include Vasse Felix, Moss Wood, Cullen Wines, Leeuwin Estate and Cape Mentelle.

What is Margaret River most famous for?

Cabernet Sauvignon and Chardonnay above all. Its mild, ocean-moderated climate and gravelly soils give structured, refined wines often compared to fine Bordeaux and Burgundy.
