Campania Wine

Campania, the region around Naples in southern Italy, is a treasure trove of ancient native grapes. It is famous for the structured, age-worthy red Taurasi (made from the firm, tannic Aglianico grape) and for distinctive, mineral whites: nutty, honeyed Fiano, citrus-and-stone Greco (the grape behind Greco di Tufo), and fresh, fruity Falanghina.

How Campania wine tastes

Campania is a paradise of ancient native grapes, and its reds and whites taste quite unlike the Italian mainstream. The great red, Taurasi, made from Aglianico, is powerful and savoury: dark cherry and plum fruit, notes of leather, tobacco and earth, high acidity and firm, grippy tannins that need time to mellow. It has earned the nickname the ‘Barolo of the South’.

The whites are just as distinctive. Fiano is textured and nutty, with pear, honey and a smoky mineral edge; Greco is fuller and citrussy with stone fruit and a stony bite; and Falanghina is the fresh, floral, citrus-driven crowd-pleaser.

What ties them together is a lively acidity and a mineral, sometimes smoky, character that comes from the region’s volcanic soils.

Signature styles & what to look for

The benchmark red is Taurasi DOCG, an Aglianico wine from the inland Irpinia hills that is built to age; a Riserva sees even longer maturing. For a gentler introduction to the grape, look for wines simply labelled Aglianico or Irpinia Aglianico.

Among whites, the two great names are Fiano di Avellino and Greco di Tufo, both DOCG wines from Irpinia that can develop beautifully with a few years in bottle. Falanghina is the easy-drinking everyday white, bright and floral.

You will also find lovely coastal and island wines, from the Amalfi Coast to Vesuvius, so it is worth exploring beyond the famous four.

The grapes & the land

Campania leans on native grapes: the red Aglianico, and the whites Fiano, Greco and Falanghina. These varieties have grown here since antiquity and are rarely seen at this quality elsewhere.

Aglianico is a late ripener, hanging on the vine well into autumn. The warm Mediterranean sun of the region ripens it fully, building the firm tannins and dark, savoury fruit that let Taurasi reward years of ageing.

The whites owe their freshness to altitude and volcanic soils. Cooler, higher inland vineyards around Avellino and the ash-rich ground near Mount Vesuvius give the wines a smoky, mineral edge and keep their acidity crisp, even under the southern sun.

Producers to know in Campania

1

Mastroberardino

Historic Irpinia estate credited with saving Aglianico and Fiano, and a benchmark name for Taurasi.

2

Feudi di San Gregorio

Leading modern Irpinia producer working across Taurasi, Fiano di Avellino and Greco di Tufo.

3

Terredora Di Paolo

Family estate with extensive Irpinia vineyards, known for native-grape reds and whites since 1978.

4

Quintodecimo

Acclaimed boutique Irpinia winery producing polished Taurasi, Fiano and Greco.

5

Villa Matilde

Respected producer known for reviving the ancient Falerno del Massico wines alongside Aglianico and Falanghina.

Food pairing. Structured Taurasi is made for rich meats: roast lamb, braises, ragù and aged cheeses that soften its tannins. The whites shine with seafood, buffalo mozzarella and vegetable dishes, with fuller Fiano and Greco able to handle richer plates.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Campania wine taste?

The flagship red, Taurasi from Aglianico, is firm and savoury with dark fruit, earthy notes and grippy tannins that soften with age. The whites are fresh and mineral, from nutty Fiano to citrussy Greco and floral Falanghina.

Which wineries is Campania known for?

Well-known producers include Mastroberardino, Feudi di San Gregorio, Terredora Di Paolo, Quintodecimo and Villa Matilde, many based in the inland Irpinia hills.

What is Taurasi wine?

Taurasi is Campania’s most celebrated red, a firm, age-worthy wine made from the Aglianico grape in the Irpinia hills. Its power and tannins have earned it the nickname the ‘Barolo of the South’.
