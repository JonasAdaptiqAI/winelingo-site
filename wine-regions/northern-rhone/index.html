How Northern Rhone wine tastes

This is the spiritual home of Syrah, and the northern Rhone style is savoury and complex rather than simply fruity. At its heart is dark fruit, blackberry, blackcurrant and dark plum, but what makes it distinctive is everything around that fruit.

Expect a signature note of black pepper, along with smoked or cured meat (often called bacon fat or charcuterie), black olive, dried herbs and a floral lift of violets. Some wines have an almost savoury, meaty, gamey side that fans adore.

The wines are medium to full-bodied with firm but fine tannins and refreshing acidity, so they feel structured and savoury rather than sweet or heavy. Young examples can be tightly wound and peppery; with age they turn silkier and more perfumed. The region also makes small amounts of gorgeous white wine from Viognier, Marsanne and Roussanne.

Signature styles & what to look for

Northern Rhone reds are essentially pure Syrah (a splash of white grapes is traditionally allowed in some appellations, co-fermented for perfume). Rather than ageing tiers, quality here is signalled by the appellation, the specific named place on the label.

The grand names for red are Cote-Rotie (elegant, perfumed, sometimes with a little Viognier), Hermitage (powerful and long-lived) and Cornas (dark, muscular, 100% Syrah). Crozes-Hermitage and Saint-Joseph cover a large area and offer the most approachable, everyday drinking, a great place to start.

For whites, look to Condrieu and Chateau-Grillet for lush, aromatic Viognier, and to Saint-Peray for whites from Marsanne and Roussanne. As a rule of thumb, the smaller and steeper the named appellation, the more serious (and pricey) the wine.

The grapes & the land

Syrah is the only red grape, joined by the white trio of Viognier, Marsanne and Roussanne. The defining feature of the land is granite: steep, terraced hillsides of decomposed granite plunging down to the Rhone river, so precipitous that much of the work is still done by hand.

The climate is continental, with warm summers and a cold edge, and the cold northerly wind, the mistral, that funnels down the valley to keep the vines healthy and the acidity fresh. Sun-drenched south-facing slopes ripen the Syrah fully while the altitude and wind preserve freshness and that peppery lift.

It is this combination, granite soils, steep sun-facing slopes and a cool continental climate, that gives northern Rhone Syrah its unmistakable mix of ripe dark fruit, spice and savoury, smoky complexity.

Producers to know in Northern Rhône

1

E. Guigal

Cote-Rotie house world-famous for its single-vineyard La La wines and a benchmark across the northern Rhone.

2

M. Chapoutier

Major biodynamic producer with holdings in Hermitage and beyond, known for its terroir-focused single-vineyard reds.

3

Paul Jaboulet Aine

Historic house and Hermitage specialist, famed for its La Chapelle bottling.

4

Domaine Jean-Louis Chave

Revered family domaine making some of the most sought-after Hermitage, red and white.

5

Domaine Jamet

Cote-Rotie grower celebrated for classically structured, aromatic Syrah from many small parcels.

Food pairing. A classic with roast and grilled meats, peppered steak, lamb and game such as venison; the smoky, savoury character also loves barbecue, sausages and cured charcuterie.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Northern Rhone wine taste?

Savoury, structured Syrah with dark blackberry and blackcurrant fruit, black pepper, smoked meat, olive and violets. It has firm tannins and fresh acidity, tasting spicy and wild when young and more perfumed with age.

Which producers is the Northern Rhone known for?

The big three are Guigal, Chapoutier and Paul Jaboulet Aine, alongside revered growers such as Jean-Louis Chave and Domaine Jamet.

What is the difference between Northern and Southern Rhone?

Northern Rhone reds are almost pure Syrah from steep granite slopes and taste savoury and peppery. Southern Rhone, such as Chateauneuf-du-Pape, is warmer and mostly Grenache-based blends that taste riper and spicier.
