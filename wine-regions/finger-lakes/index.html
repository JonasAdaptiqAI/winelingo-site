Wine region

Finger Lakes Wine

A cool-climate region in upstate New York set around deep glacial lakes, regarded as one of America’s best spots for Riesling. It produces zesty Rieslings from bone-dry to sweet, plus growing quality in cool-climate reds and sparkling wine.

Country
United States
World
New World
Climate
Cool continental, lake-moderated
Key grapes
Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Cabernet Franc, Chardonnay

Finger Lakes Riesling is all about zingy, mouth-watering acidity wrapped around lime, green apple and orchard fruit, often with a faint whiff of petrol or wet stone as it ages. It runs the full sweetness range, from bone-dry and crisp to gently off-dry to lusciously sweet, but almost always stays light-bodied, low in alcohol and refreshing.

How Finger Lakes wine tastes

The signature here is bright, juicy acidity. Because the growing season is cool, grapes ripen slowly and keep their natural tartness, so the wines feel crisp and lively rather than heavy or warm.

Riesling is the star. Expect flavours of lime, lemon, green apple and pear, sometimes a touch of white peach or apricot, and a mineral, almost saline streak that people often describe as ‘wet stone’. Older bottles develop a distinctive kerosene or petrol note, which sounds off-putting but is prized by Riesling lovers as a sign of graceful ageing.

Crucially, sweetness varies a lot from bottle to bottle. A wine can be bone-dry (no perceptible sugar), gently off-dry (a touch of sweetness to soften the acidity), or fully dessert-sweet. Many labels now print a little dryness scale on the back to help you choose.

Beyond Riesling you’ll find perfumed Gewürztraminer, cool-climate Chardonnay, delicate Pinot Noir, peppery Cabernet Franc and a growing range of crisp traditional-method sparkling wines.

Signature styles & what to look for

Dry Riesling is the calling card: taut, citrusy and refreshing. Off-dry (semi-dry) Riesling keeps a little sweetness to balance the high acidity and is very food-friendly. At the sweet end you’ll find late-harvest and ice wine Rieslings, where grapes are picked very late (or frozen on the vine) to concentrate the sugars.

There is no ageing-tier classification the way Rioja or Bordeaux have, so the label leans on grape and sweetness level instead. Look for the words ‘dry’, ‘semi-dry’, ‘semi-sweet’ or a dryness scale, and for single-vineyard bottlings, which are usually a producer’s most serious wines.

Also worth seeking out: aromatic Gewürztraminer, savoury Cabernet Franc, and sparkling wines made in the traditional (Champagne) method.

The grapes & the land

The region sits in upstate New York around a set of long, deep glacial lakes. Those lakes are the whole secret: they store summer warmth and release it slowly into autumn and winter, buffering the vines against frost in what would otherwise be a punishingly cold continental climate. The best vineyards hug the steep slopes right above the water.

Riesling thrives here precisely because it loves a cool, slow ripening season, which preserves its trademark high acidity and pure fruit. Other cool-climate grapes such as Gewürztraminer, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Cabernet Franc suit the same conditions. The soils are a patchwork of shale, slate and glacial deposits that add to the wines’ stony, mineral character.

Producers to know in Finger Lakes

1

Dr. Konstantin Frank

Founded 1962 on Keuka Lake; the pioneer who proved European vinifera grapes like Riesling could thrive here.

2

Fox Run Vineyards

Seneca Lake estate producing acclaimed dry and off-dry Rieslings since 1990.

3

Ravines Wine Cellars

Known for taut, lemony, mineral-driven dry Rieslings.

4

Red Newt Cellars

Long-running Seneca Lake winery making benchmark, well-priced Rieslings.

5

Weis Vineyards

German-rooted producer respected for its Rieslings, including late-harvest and ice-wine styles.

Food pairing. Off-dry Riesling is a natural partner for spicy Thai, Indian or Sichuan food, cutting the heat with its sweetness and acidity. Dry versions love fresh fish, oysters, roast chicken and salads, while sweet late-harvest bottles shine with fruit tarts or blue cheese.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Finger Lakes wine taste?

Mostly crisp, light and refreshing, led by Riesling with lime, green apple and orchard-fruit flavours over lively acidity. Sweetness ranges from bone-dry to dessert-sweet, so always check the label.

Which wineries is Finger Lakes known for?

Dr. Konstantin Frank (the vinifera pioneer), Fox Run Vineyards, Ravines Wine Cellars, Red Newt Cellars and Weis Vineyards are all well-established names for Riesling.

Is Finger Lakes Riesling sweet or dry?

It can be either. The region makes everything from bone-dry to lusciously sweet Riesling, and many producers now print a dryness scale on the back label so you know what you’re getting.
