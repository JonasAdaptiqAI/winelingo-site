Douro Wine Region

The Douro is the historic home of Port, the fortified sweet wine, and increasingly produces powerful, concentrated dry red table wines from vineyards across the same region.

Country
Portugal
World
Old World
Climate

Key grapes
Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz, Tinta Barroca

The Douro makes two great things: Port, the rich, sweet fortified wine ranging from fruity Ruby to nutty aged Tawny, and increasingly bold, concentrated dry reds full of dark berry, schist minerality and firm tannins. Both are deep, powerful and built to last.

Five things to know about Douro

  1. Its boundaries were carved in granite in 1756. The Marquis of Pombal drew the Douro on a map and then had it marked on the ground. In all, 335 of these marcos pombalinos were set, and many of them still stand among the vines today.
  2. Over a hundred grapes are legal; five do the work. Port regulations sanction more than a hundred varieties, yet Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz, Tinta Barroca and Tinto Cão carry almost all of it. The oldest vineyards are still planted as a jumbled field blend.
  3. Only 18 per cent of the Douro is vineyard. The demarcated region covers about 250,000 hectares, of which 43,708 are under vine. The rest is rock, scrub and river. The vines take only the slopes that will ripen fruit properly.
  4. The average Douro farmer works two hectares. Around 20,000 growers share the region, most of them on plots of roughly two hectares each. The grand Port houses are built on fruit bought in from thousands of tiny hillside smallholdings.
  5. UNESCO listed the landscape in 2001, not the wine. The Alto Douro was inscribed as a cultural landscape: the terraces, the dry-stone walls and the quintas are the heritage. Wine has been made on those slopes for some 2,000 years.

How Douro wine tastes

The Douro is the home of Port — a fortified wine, meaning grape spirit is added during fermentation to stop it and leave natural sweetness behind. Styles range from young, fruity Ruby and richer Vintage Port (blackberry, cherry, chocolate) to Tawny, aged in barrel until it turns nutty and mellow with flavours of dried fig, walnut and toffee.

From the very same terraces come the region’s dry red table wines, which have surged in reputation. These are deep and concentrated, with blackberry and dark plum, a floral and herbal lift, a stony schist minerality and firm, ripe tannins.

Both styles share the Douro’s signature intensity — a product of small, thick-skinned berries baked on steep, hot slopes. The best examples, Port and dry red alike, can age for decades.

Signature styles & what to look for

Port comes in several styles worth knowing: Ruby and Reserve (young and fruity), Late Bottled Vintage or LBV (a single year, ready to drink), Tawny with an age indication such as 10, 20, 30 or 40 years (barrel-aged and nutty), and Vintage Port (from a single outstanding year, bottled young and made to age for decades). There are white and rosé Ports too.

For dry wines, look for ”Douro” on the label (as opposed to “Porto”); the top reds are often single-quinta (single-estate) bottlings or a producer’s flagship blend.

The Douro is the world’s oldest demarcated wine region, first marked out in the early 18th century.

The grapes & the land

The classic dark grapes are Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz (Tempranillo), Tinta Barroca and Tinto Cão, usually blended together for both Port and dry reds.

Steep terraces of schist — a hard, layered rock — line the Douro River, baking under hot, dry summers that ripen grapes to high sugar and concentration. The poor, fractured schist drains well, holds heat and lets vine roots dive deep in search of water, stressing the vines into low yields of small, intense berries.

Centuries of tradition, including foot-treading grapes in stone lagares and fortifying with grape spirit to halt fermentation, shaped the rich, sweet, age-worthy style that defines classic Port.

Producers to know in Douro

1

Taylor’s (Taylor Fladgate)

Historic Port house renowned for its Vintage Ports and the single-estate Quinta de Vargellas.

2

Graham’s

Long-established Port house, part of the Symington family estates, famed for rich Vintage and aged Tawny Ports.

3

Niepoort

Family Port house and a pioneer of the Douro’s modern dry reds, based at Quinta de Nápoles.

4

Casa Ferreirinha

Maker of Barca Velha, Portugal’s most iconic dry red, first produced in 1952.

5

Quinta do Noval

Historic Douro estate and Port house, famous for its rare Nacional Vintage Port.

Food pairing. Vintage Port is a classic with blue cheese, especially Stilton, or with dark chocolate; nutty Tawny suits caramel and nut desserts. The powerful dry reds are made for roast and grilled meats, game and rich stews.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Douro wine taste?

The Douro is famous for Port — rich and sweet, from fruity Ruby to nutty aged Tawny — and for bold, concentrated dry reds full of dark berry, stony minerality and firm tannins. Both styles are deep, powerful and long-lived.

Which producers is the Douro known for?

Famous Port houses include Taylor’s, Graham’s and Quinta do Noval; Niepoort and Casa Ferreirinha (maker of the iconic Barca Velha) are leaders for dry red wines.

What is the difference between Port and Douro wine?

Both come from the same region. Port is fortified — grape spirit is added to leave it sweet and higher in alcohol — while wines labelled ‘Douro’ are dry, unfortified table wines made from the same grapes.
