Martinborough Wine Region

A small, high-quality region at the southern tip of New Zealand’s North Island, best known for concentrated, savoury, often dense and earthy boutique Pinot Noir.

Country

Climate

Dense, savoury Pinot Noir with dark cherry and plum fruit, a distinctive earthy, dried-herb, almost gamey edge, and firm structure. It feels more brooding and concentrated than fruity, with fine tannins and fresh acidity.

How Martinborough wine tastes

Martinborough Pinot Noir has a reputation for being one of New Zealand’s most savoury and structured. Alongside the dark cherry and plum fruit you often find earthy, mushroomy, dried-herb and spice notes, and sometimes a wild, gamey character that gives the wine real personality.

It tends to be denser and more serious than the perfumed, red-fruited style of some other regions. The tannins are fine but present, giving the wine grip and a savoury ‘chew’, and there is bright acidity underneath keeping everything fresh.

These are wines built to be considered rather than gulped. Many drink well young but reward a few years in the cellar, where the fruit softens and those earthy, complex notes deepen.

Signature styles & what to look for

Pinot Noir is the signature and the reason to seek the region out: concentrated, savoury and age-worthy, usually matured in French oak barrels for texture and gentle spice. Because so many estates are small, bottlings are often site-specific and made in limited quantities.

Martinborough is a boutique region rather than a volume one, so ‘what to look for’ is really the name of a good grower. There are no formal ageing tiers as in Rioja; quality is expressed through single-vineyard and reserve cuvees from individual estates.

The region also does aromatic whites very well. Keep an eye out for its crisp, textured Pinot Gris, dry Riesling and characterful Sauvignon Blanc, which is often rounder and less overtly tropical than Marlborough’s.

The grapes & the land

Pinot Noir dominates the plantings, grown on the free-draining gravel terraces around the town of Martinborough in the Wairarapa, at the southern tip of the North Island.

The climate is cool and maritime but with a continental streak: low rainfall, warm days and cool nights, plus drying autumn winds. Those old river-terrace gravels drain fast and keep yields low, which concentrates the fruit.

The combination of a long, dry ripening season and naturally small crops is what gives the Pinot its depth, structure and savoury complexity rather than simple sweet fruit. It is a small area, so the best sites and growers matter a great deal.

Producers to know in Martinborough

1

Ata Rangi

A founding estate and long-regarded benchmark for dense, age-worthy Martinborough Pinot Noir.

2

Dry River

Pioneering boutique winery, established 1979, prized for tightly structured, long-lived Pinot Noir and aromatic whites.

3

Palliser Estate

One of the region’s larger, consistently well-respected producers of Pinot Noir and Sauvignon Blanc.

4

Escarpment

Founded by Pinot specialist Larry McKenna, known for single-vineyard Martinborough Pinot Noir.

5

Kusuda

Highly sought-after small-production estate making concentrated, Burgundian-styled Pinot Noir and Riesling.

Food pairing. Made for roast duck, mushroom risotto, game birds, lamb and earthy dishes with beetroot or lentils; the savoury edge loves anything a touch gamey.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Martinborough wine taste?

Martinborough is best known for savoury, structured Pinot Noir with dark cherry and plum fruit and an earthy, dried-herb, sometimes gamey character. It is denser and more serious than many New Zealand Pinots, with fine tannins and fresh acidity.

Which wineries is Martinborough known for?

Leading Martinborough producers include Ata Rangi, Dry River, Palliser Estate, Escarpment and Kusuda. Ata Rangi and Dry River were among the pioneers that established the region’s Pinot Noir reputation.

Is Martinborough the same as Marlborough?

No, and they are easy to confuse. Martinborough is a small Pinot Noir region in the Wairarapa on the North Island, while Marlborough is a much larger Sauvignon Blanc region on the South Island.
