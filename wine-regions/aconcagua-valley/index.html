Aconcagua Valley Wine Region

A historic valley north of Santiago, named after South America’s highest peak, long home to some of Chile’s most acclaimed reds.

Country
Chile
World
New World
Climate
Warm Mediterranean with cool coastal pockets
Key grapes
Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah, Carménère, Sauvignon Blanc

Aconcagua reds are ripe, deep and firmly structured: Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah full of blackcurrant, blackberry and dark plum, with a savoury, peppery edge and fine, grippy tannins. Cooler coastal sites also give fresher, more elegant Syrah and crisp whites.

Five things to know about Aconcagua Valley

  1. Seña beat Château Lafite in Berlin. On 23 January 2004 Eduardo Chadwick put his wines blind against Bordeaux first growths and Tuscan icons in front of 36 European critics, the tasting led by Steven Spurrier. Seña 2001 came second, and a Chilean wine came first.
  2. The valley began with one man and one hillside. Don Maximiano Errázuriz Valdivieso planted at Panquehue in 1870, on ground everyone else judged too poor and too dry. His estate is still the reference point for Aconcagua reds.
  3. It is one of Chile’s smallest important valleys. Aconcagua has roughly 1,000 hectares under vine. Colchagua, further south, has about 30,000. Reputation here rests on a handful of estates rather than on volume.
  4. Costa and Andes on the label are not decoration. Chile added those east-west terms in 2011. Aconcagua Costa sits at 100 to 400 metres near the Pacific and gives taut Syrah and whites; Aconcagua Andes climbs to 1,200 metres for the structured reds.
  5. The valley is named after a mountain in another country. Aconcagua rises 6,961 metres on the Argentine side of the border, the highest peak outside Asia. Its snowmelt feeds the river that irrigates these vineyards, and cold air draining off the range keeps the reds fresh.

How Aconcagua Valley wine tastes

This is red-wine country first and foremost. The warm, sunny inland vineyards ripen Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah fully, so the wines are deeply coloured and concentrated, brimming with blackcurrant, blackberry and dark plum. Underneath the fruit there’s a savoury, sometimes peppery or herbal note, and a backbone of firm but polished tannins that gives them real shape and ageing potential.

What stops these wines from feeling heavy is altitude and mountain air. Cool nights and breezes coming off the Andes preserve freshness, so even the biggest reds keep a welcome lift of acidity.

Closer to the Pacific, in the cooler coastal pockets, the style shifts. Syrah becomes more perfumed and elegant, and there are taut, mineral whites from Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay.

Signature styles & what to look for

The valley’s calling card is structured, age-worthy Cabernet Sauvignon, often the heart of a Bordeaux-style blend with Carménère and Syrah. These are the wines that first earned Aconcagua its reputation for reds able to stand alongside the world’s best.

Syrah is the other star, made in two guises: rich and dark from the warm interior, fresher and more savoury from cooler sites. You’ll also see the terms Aconcagua Costa (coast) for cooler, fresher wines and Aconcagua Andes for the mountain vineyards, both useful clues to a wine’s style. Chile’s signature grape Carménère adds a plummy, herbal note to many blends.

The grapes & the land

Cabernet Sauvignon leads, with Syrah, Carménère and, in the cool spots, Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay. The valley runs from the Andes down to the Pacific, and that geography explains everything.

Inland it’s hot, dry and very sunny, ideal for ripening thick-skinned reds, while meltwater and cold air draining from the mountains (the valley is named after Aconcagua, the highest peak in the Americas) keep the vines from baking and lock in acidity. Near the coast, morning fog and ocean breezes cool things dramatically, which is why that end of the valley makes the fresher, finer styles.

Producers to know in Aconcagua Valley

1

Viña Errázuriz

The valley’s founding estate (established 1870), famous for its flagship Don Maximiano and single-vineyard Las Pizarras wines.

2

Seña

Icon Chilean red begun in 1995 as an Andes-to-coast Bordeaux-style blend, now owned by the Chadwick family.

3

Viña von Siebenthal

Boutique Panquehue estate founded by Swiss-born Mauro von Siebenthal, known for concentrated, structured reds.

4

Flaherty Wines

Small family winery making well-regarded, hand-crafted red blends from Aconcagua fruit.

Food pairing. These are wines for the grill: Chilean-style barbecued beef and lamb, chunky steaks and rich stews all stand up to the reds’ structure. The fresher coastal Syrah and whites suit roast chicken, salmon and seafood.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Aconcagua Valley wine taste?

Mostly rich, deep red wine: ripe blackcurrant and blackberry fruit, a savoury peppery edge and firm, fine tannins, all kept fresh by cool mountain air. Cooler coastal sites give lighter, more elegant Syrah and crisp whites.

Which wineries is Aconcagua Valley known for?

Viña Errázuriz is the historic name that put the valley on the map, and its sister label Seña is one of Chile’s icon reds. Smaller estates such as Von Siebenthal and Flaherty Wines are also well worth seeking out.

Is Aconcagua Valley wine good value?

It can be excellent value at the everyday level, but the region is also home to some of Chile’s most ambitious (and pricier) icon reds. You’ll find both ends of the scale under the Aconcagua name.
