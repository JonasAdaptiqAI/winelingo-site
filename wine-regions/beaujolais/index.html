Beaujolais Wine Region

A region in eastern France just south of Burgundy famous for light, fruity, low-tannin red wines made almost entirely from the Gamay grape.

Country
France
World
Old World
Climate
Semi-continental (with some Mediterranean influence)
Key grapes
Gamay, Chardonnay

Light, juicy reds bursting with fresh red fruit like cherry and raspberry, with soft tannins, lively acidity and often a floral or slightly earthy edge. Most are gulpable and best enjoyed young, sometimes lightly chilled.

How Beaujolais wine tastes

Picture crunchy red cherries and raspberries with a splash of violets. Beaujolais is one of the most easy-going red wines there is: pale to medium in colour, light-bodied, low in the grippy tannins that dry your mouth, and refreshed by bright acidity that keeps everything lively.

The style you meet depends on where in the region the wine comes from. Simple Beaujolais and Beaujolais Nouveau are all about immediate, primary fruit, sometimes with a playful note of banana or bubblegum that comes from the winemaking. The ten named villages known as the crus give deeper, more serious wines with real structure and a stony, mineral undertone.

Because the tannins are so gentle, a slight chill of twenty minutes in the fridge suits most Beaujolais beautifully and makes it even more thirst-quenching.

Signature styles & what to look for

There are three quality levels to know. Beaujolais (the basic tier) is light and fruity for everyday drinking. Beaujolais-Villages comes from a group of better hillside communes and offers a bit more depth. The ten crus are the top rung, each its own appellation: Brouilly, Côte de Brouilly, Régnié, Morgon, Chiroubles, Fleurie, Moulin-à-Vent, Chénas, Juliénas and Saint-Amour.

Among the crus, Fleurie is prized for perfume and finesse, while Morgon and Moulin-à-Vent are the fullest and most ageworthy, sometimes taking on a darker, almost Pinot-like character with a few years in bottle.

You will also see Beaujolais Nouveau, released on the third Thursday of November just weeks after harvest. It is a fun, grapey celebration of the vintage rather than a wine to keep. A little white Beaujolais made from Chardonnay also exists, though it is rare.

The grapes & the land

Beaujolais is almost entirely Gamay, a thin-skinned grape that naturally gives pale, fruity, low-tannin reds. A dab of Chardonnay accounts for the small amount of white wine.

Much of the vivid fruit also comes from a technique called carbonic (or semi-carbonic) maceration, where whole bunches of grapes begin fermenting inside their own skins before being pressed. This coaxes out bright, juicy flavours and keeps tannins soft; it is what gives Nouveau its playful banana-and-bubblegum lift, though cru producers use it more gently to build wines that can age.

The north of the region, where the crus sit, is built on granite and schist soils that lend minerality and backbone, while the flatter south has heavier clay and limestone that suits the lighter styles. The semi-continental climate ripens Gamay reliably yet holds on to the freshness that makes these wines so drinkable.

Producers to know in Beaujolais

1

Georges Duboeuf

The region’s best-known négociant, central to popularising Beaujolais Nouveau and the crus worldwide.

2

Jean Foillard

Revered Morgon grower and a leading figure in Beaujolais’s natural, low-intervention wine movement.

3

Marcel Lapierre

Pioneering Morgon estate, one of the ‘Gang of Four’ who revived traditional, chemical-free winemaking.

4

Château Thivin

The oldest estate on Mont Brouilly, run by the Geoffray family, known for structured Côte de Brouilly.

5

Jean-Paul Thévenet

Another of the ‘Gang of Four’, making expressive, old-vine Morgon from organically farmed fruit.

Food pairing. A wonderfully food-friendly red for charcuterie, roast chicken, pork, sausages and picnic spreads. Lighter styles love a slight chill and pair happily with a wide range of everyday dishes.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Beaujolais wine taste?

Light, juicy and fruity, with fresh cherry and raspberry flavours, soft tannins and bright acidity. Most are easy to drink and refreshing, especially with a slight chill.

Which wineries is Beaujolais known for?

Well-known names include Georges Duboeuf, Jean Foillard, Marcel Lapierre, Château Thivin and Jean-Paul Thévenet, several of whom helped revive the region’s serious, traditional wines.

What is the difference between Beaujolais Nouveau and cru Beaujolais?

Beaujolais Nouveau is a young, grapey wine released just weeks after harvest for early drinking. Cru Beaujolais comes from ten top villages and offers more structure, depth and the ability to age.
