Santorini Wine Region

A dramatic volcanic island in the Aegean famous for bone-dry, intensely mineral white wines from the Assyrtiko grape, plus Vinsanto, a luscious sun-dried sweet wine.

How Santorini wine tastes

Santorini’s dry whites, built on the Assyrtiko grape, are some of the most distinctive in the world. They are bone-dry and bracingly fresh, with citrus fruit, a stony, flinty mineral quality and an unmistakable salty, sea-spray tang that comes from the island’s volcanic soils and coastal setting.

What sets Assyrtiko apart is that it keeps ferocious acidity even in a hot climate, so the wine feels taut and electric while still having real weight and body on the palate. It is savoury and mineral rather than fruity, and it often carries a smoky, almost saline finish that lingers.

At the other extreme, the island’s traditional Vinsanto is a luscious sweet wine: amber-coloured, and tasting of dried figs, raisins, caramel and orange peel, yet kept lively by that same bright acidity.

Signature styles & what to look for

The two icons are dry white Santorini (Assyrtiko-based) and sweet Vinsanto. A bottle labelled simply Santorini is the dry white, at least 75% Assyrtiko and usually higher, often with a splash of the local Athiri or Aidani.

You will also see Nykteri, a richer, more powerful traditional style of dry white, historically picked very ripe and often barrel-aged, giving more texture and a fuller feel. Vinsanto is made from sun-dried grapes and, to carry the name, is aged for a minimum of two years in barrel, though the best spend far longer and grow deep and complex.

Styles range from crisp, steel-fermented whites to oak-aged and old-vine bottlings with extra depth. Because yields are tiny, Santorini is rarely a cheap wine, but the intensity in the glass explains why.

The grapes & the land

Assyrtiko leads, with Athiri and Aidani as aromatic partners. Santorini is a volcanic island in the Aegean, and its soils, ash, pumice and volcanic rock with almost no clay, hold very little water or nutrients, forcing tiny yields and lending the wines their smoky, saline, flinty signature.

Fierce winds and next to no rainfall make this one of the harshest places on earth to grow vines. Growers train the plants into a low, coiled basket shape called kouloura, weaving the canes into a nest that shields the grapes from sun and wind and helps capture precious morning moisture. Ungrafted, often very old vines and this extreme environment are exactly why Assyrtiko here keeps its searing acidity and mineral bite.

Producers to know in Santorini

1

Domaine Sigalas

Leading estate founded by Paris Sigalas, key to introducing Santorini Assyrtiko to the wider world.

2

Argyros Estate

Family estate dating to 1903, known for old-vine Assyrtiko and long-aged Vinsanto.

3

Artemis Karamolegos

One of the island’s most important modern wineries, with a broad range of Assyrtiko.

4

Boutari

Historic Greek wine company whose Santorini winery focuses on the island’s native whites.

5

Gavalas

One of the oldest family estates on Santorini, working indigenous varieties and traditional methods.

Food pairing. The dry whites are made for the sea: grilled fish and octopus, oysters, calamari, and the island’s own tomato fritters and fava. Sweet Vinsanto is lovely with dried fruit, nutty desserts or a strong, salty cheese.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Santorini wine taste?

The dry whites are bone-dry and intensely mineral, with lemon and lime, a smoky, flinty edge and a salty, saline tang. They have searing acidity and more body than most whites, finishing long and savoury.

Which wineries is Santorini known for?

Respected producers include Domaine Sigalas, Argyros Estate, Artemis Karamolegos, Boutari and Gavalas.

What is Vinsanto?

Vinsanto is Santorini’s traditional sweet wine, made from sun-dried Assyrtiko and other local grapes and aged for at least two years in barrel. It is rich and honeyed, tasting of figs, raisins and caramel, but stays balanced thanks to Assyrtiko’s high acidity.
