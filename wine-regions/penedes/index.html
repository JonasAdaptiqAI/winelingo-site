Penedès Wine

A versatile Catalan region near Barcelona, best known as the heartland of Cava, Spain’s bottle-fermented sparkling wine (made the same way as Champagne), and spanning crisp sparkling and still whites at altitude to riper reds nearer the coast.

Country

World

Climate

Key grapes

Penedès is best known for Cava — dry, bottle-fermented sparkling wine with green apple, citrus and a gentle nutty, bready note from time on its lees. Its still wines range from crisp, fresh whites to riper, rounder reds nearer the warm coast.

How Penedès wine tastes

The wine most people reach for from Penedès is Cava, Spain’s traditional-method sparkling wine — made in the same painstaking way as Champagne, with a second fermentation inside the bottle. Expect a fine, persistent bead, flavours of green apple, lemon and pear, and a savoury, slightly bready or nutty character that grows with age on the lees (the spent yeast the wine rests on).

The still whites are typically crisp and refreshing, with citrus, orchard fruit and a clean finish. Xarel·lo, one of the local grapes, can also give richer, more textured dry whites with a distinctive earthy, herbal streak.

The reds vary with where they grow. Cooler hillside sites give fresher, medium-bodied styles, while warmer coastal vineyards produce riper, rounder reds from both local and international grapes.

Signature styles & what to look for

For sparkling wine, the key is ageing time, and Cava has a clear ladder. Cava de Guarda is the fresh, youngest style. Cava de Guarda Superior covers the more serious tiers: Reserva (aged at least 18 months on its lees), Gran Reserva (at least 30 months) and the top single-estate Cava de Paraje Calificado. As a rule, the longer the ageing, the deeper and more toasty-complex the wine.

A growing number of the region’s finest sparkling estates now bottle under Corpinnat or as Clàssic Penedès rather than the Cava name — different labels, but the same painstaking bottle-fermented method and often very long ageing.

For still wines, look to the higher, cooler vineyards for crisp whites and to the warmer sites for the fuller reds.

The grapes & the land

Penedès sits in Catalonia just inland from Barcelona, and its great advantage is range of altitude. Vineyards climb from warm, sunny coastal flats up to cool, breezy hills several hundred metres above the sea.

That spread lets growers pick high-acid grapes for sparkling wine as well as riper fruit for reds, all within one region. Mediterranean sun provides the ripeness; sea breezes and cool highland nights preserve the freshness that good Cava depends on.

The classic Cava trio is native: Macabeo for fruit and body, Xarel·lo for structure and earthy character, and Parellada for delicacy and floral lift. Chardonnay and Pinot Noir also feature in sparkling blends, while grapes such as Grenache (Garnacha) and Cabernet Sauvignon fill out the still reds.

Producers to know in Penedès

1

Codorníu

One of the oldest sparkling wine houses in the world and a founding pioneer of traditional-method Cava, based in Sant Sadurní d’Anoia.

2

Freixenet

Large, globally recognised Cava house, maker of the widely exported Cordon Negro.

3

Gramona

Family estate famed for exceptionally long-aged, complex traditional-method sparkling wine (now bottled under Corpinnat).

4

Familia Torres

Historic Penedès family firm known for characterful still wines, from crisp whites to ambitious Cabernet-based reds like Mas La Plana.

5

Parés Baltà

Family-run, organically and biodynamically farmed estate producing both Cava and still wines.

Food pairing. Dry Cava is a brilliant all-rounder — try it with tapas, fried seafood, jamón or salty snacks — while the crisp still whites suit shellfish and the riper reds handle grilled meats and stews.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Penedès wine taste?

Its signature Cava is dry and refreshing, with green apple, citrus and a nutty, bready depth from bottle ageing. The still whites are crisp and clean, and the reds run from fresh and medium-bodied to riper and rounder near the coast.

Is Cava the same as Champagne?

It is made the same way — a second fermentation in the bottle — but with different grapes and from a warmer Spanish climate. That gives Cava its own character and, usually, a friendlier price.

Which wineries is Penedès known for?

The great Cava houses Codorníu and Freixenet, the long-ageing specialist Gramona, the still-wine leader Familia Torres and the organic estate Parés Baltà are all well-established names.
