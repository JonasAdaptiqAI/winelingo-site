Clare Valley Wine Region

A South Australian region celebrated above all for bone-dry, racy Riesling with intense lime and mineral character, alongside firm, structured Shiraz and Cabernet.

Country
Australia
World
New World
Climate

Key grapes
Riesling, Shiraz, Cabernet Sauvignon, Grenache

Above all, bone-dry Riesling with laser-like intensity: pure lime and lemon, a hint of green apple and blossom, a stony mineral streak and racy, mouth-watering acidity. Alongside it sit firm, structured Shiraz and Cabernet, ripe and dark-fruited with a sturdy backbone.

Five things to know about Clare Valley

  1. Clare killed the cork. In 2000, thirteen Clare winemakers led by Jeffrey Grosset bottled their Rieslings under Stelvin screwcap together. That single act turned the screwcap from a cheap-wine stigma into the default closure across Australia and New Zealand.
  2. Riesling is not even the most-planted grape. Of roughly 5,100 hectares, Shiraz holds about 35 per cent, with Cabernet Sauvignon and Riesling neck and neck just over 20 each. The valley is famous for a wine that fills barely a fifth of it.
  3. The acidity comes from altitude, not latitude. Clare vineyards sit between 400 and 500 metres, high enough that hot days collapse into cold nights. That swing is what leaves the lime bite in the Riesling and the grip in the Shiraz.
  4. Watervale and Polish Hill River are not appellations. Clare has five recognised districts — Auburn, Clare, Polish Hill River, Sevenhill and Watervale — and not one of them is a legal sub-region. They are conventions among growers, and still worth reading.
  5. Jesuits got here first. They planted the valley’s first vineyard at Sevenhill in the 1840s and founded Sevenhill Cellars in 1851, still the oldest winery in Clare. Altar wine, not fashion, is what first put vines in this ground.

How Clare Valley wine tastes

Clare Valley is one of the world’s great homes for dry Riesling, and its style is unmistakable: intense, zesty and precise. Forget any idea that Riesling is sweet, here it is bone-dry and racy.

The hallmark is vivid lime, fresh lime juice and zest, joined by lemon, green apple and a touch of blossom, all over a bright, mouth-watering acidity that makes the wine feel crisp and energetic. There is often a fine, stony or mineral streak running underneath. Young Clare Riesling is all about that zippy freshness.

The valley’s reds are a serious sideline. Shiraz and Cabernet Sauvignon here are ripe and dark-fruited but built with firm structure and grip, more sturdy and savoury than soft and jammy. It is the crisp, age-worthy Riesling, though, that remains the region’s calling card.

Signature styles & what to look for

Dry Riesling is the wine to start with, and it comes in two classic accents worth spotting on the label. Watervale Riesling tends to be a little softer and more floral; Polish Hill River Riesling is firmer, more mineral and often needs a touch more time.

There is no formal ageing classification, but Clare Riesling is famous for how it evolves in bottle. Young, it is all lime and zip; with five to ten years or more, it develops gorgeous notes of toast, honey and lime marmalade while staying dry and fresh. The top wines can age for twenty years or beyond.

A nice bit of trivia: Clare producers led the switch to screwcaps for Riesling, which helps keep these wines crisp and fault-free as they age. For reds, look to the region’s structured Shiraz and Cabernet.

The grapes & the land

Riesling is the signature grape and the reason to know Clare, with Shiraz, Cabernet Sauvignon and some Grenache making the sturdy reds.

The magic is in the climate. Clare has warm, dry, sunny days that ripen the fruit fully, but because the vineyards sit at altitude, the nights turn cool. That daily swing between warm days and cold nights is what preserves the high natural acidity, giving the Riesling its zesty, lime-driven freshness and its ability to age. The same continental conditions and varied soils build the firm structure and depth found in the reds.

Producers to know in Clare Valley

Grosset

Benchmark Riesling maker, best known for its Polish Hill and Springvale bottlings.

Jim Barry Wines

Family winery making Clare Riesling and Shiraz, including the noted Armagh Shiraz.

3

Family estate in the Polish Hill River subregion, well regarded for its Riesling and Shiraz.

4

Kilikanoon

Clare producer recognised for structured, age-worthy Riesling and Shiraz.

5

Sevenhill Cellars

Founded by Jesuits in 1851, the oldest winery in the Clare Valley.

Food pairing. Young, zesty Clare Riesling is superb with fresh seafood, oysters, sushi and lightly spiced Thai or Vietnamese food. The firm Shiraz and Cabernet call for grilled red meat, lamb or a hearty roast.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Clare Valley wine taste?

Its famous Riesling is bone-dry, intense and zesty, all lime and lemon with a stony mineral streak and racy acidity. The reds, Shiraz and Cabernet, are ripe but firm and structured.

Which wineries is Clare Valley known for?

Leading names include Grosset, Jim Barry, Pikes, Kilikanoon and the historic Sevenhill Cellars, the oldest winery in the valley.

Is Clare Valley Riesling sweet?

No, it is classically bone-dry. It tastes so intense with lime and citrus that people sometimes assume it is sweet, but the wine is dry, with the fruit balanced by crisp, fresh acidity.
