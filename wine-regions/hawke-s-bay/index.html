Hawke’s Bay Wine Region

New Zealand’s premier red-wine region, on the North Island’s east coast, known for full Bordeaux-style blends and Syrah, plus rich, textured Chardonnay.

Key grapes
Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah, Chardonnay

Hawke’s Bay reds are ripe but structured, offering blackcurrant, plum and dark berry fruit with firm-but-supple tannins, cedary oak and, in the Syrahs, a distinctive black-pepper and violet lift. The Chardonnays are rich and textured with stone fruit, citrus and toasty, nutty oak.

How Hawke’s Bay wine tastes

This is New Zealand’s leading region for serious reds, and they taste ripe yet refined rather than jammy. Bordeaux-style blends (mostly Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon) show blackcurrant, plum and dark berry, framed by firm but rounded tannins and a savoury, cedary edge from oak ageing.

Syrah is the region’s rising star and tastes quite different from big Australian Shiraz: think blackberry and dark plum with a signature crack of black pepper, floral violet notes and a more medium-bodied, elegant feel.

The Chardonnay is equally prized: rich and creamy but balanced, with white peach, nectarine, citrus and toasty, nutty oak. You’ll also find crisp, tropical Sauvignon Blanc and some aromatic whites, though these are more of a supporting act here than in cooler parts of the country.

Signature styles & what to look for

The headline styles are Merlot-led Bordeaux blends, standalone Syrah, and barrel-fermented Chardonnay.

There is no official ageing-tier ladder, but sub-region on the label matters a lot. The name to know is the Gimblett Gravels, a defined 800-hectare district of stony, heat-radiating soils that produces the region’s most powerful, age-worthy reds. The neighbouring Bridge Pa Triangle is also known for excellent reds, with a slightly rounder, red-fruited style.

Seeing ‘Gimblett Gravels’ or ‘Bridge Pa’ on a bottle usually signals a producer’s more ambitious red. For whites, look to the cooler coastal and hillside sites for the finer Chardonnays.

The grapes & the land

Hawke’s Bay sits on the North Island’s sunny east coast and is one of New Zealand’s warmest, sunniest wine regions. That extra warmth is what lets late-ripening Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah reach full ripeness, where cooler parts of the country cannot.

The celebrated Gimblett Gravels were exposed when a river changed course after an 1867 flood, leaving deep, free-draining gravel and stone. These soils store the day’s heat and release it back to the vines, concentrating the fruit and giving the reds their ripe depth and structure.

Chardonnay rounds out the mix, doing especially well on the region’s clay and limestone-influenced sites, where it keeps freshness alongside its richness.

Producers to know in Hawke’s Bay

1

Te Mata Estate

One of New Zealand’s oldest wineries (est. 1896), famed for its long-lived ‘Coleraine’ Bordeaux-style red.

2

Craggy Range

Acclaimed producer of single-vineyard Gimblett Gravels reds and Syrah.

3

Trinity Hill

A Gimblett Gravels pioneer, celebrated for its Syrah and Bordeaux blends.

4

Church Road

Historic Hawke’s Bay winery with a long track record in reds and Chardonnay.

5

Stonecroft

The original planter of Syrah in the Gimblett Gravels and a specialist in the grape.

Food pairing. The Bordeaux blends love roast lamb, beef and other red meats, while the peppery Syrah is a natural with grilled or braised dishes and game. Save the rich, toasty Chardonnay for roast chicken, pork or creamy seafood dishes.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How does Hawke’s Bay wine taste?

The reds are ripe but structured, with blackcurrant and plum in the Bordeaux blends and blackberry plus black pepper in the Syrah. The Chardonnays are rich and textured with stone fruit and toasty oak.

Which wineries is Hawke’s Bay known for?

Te Mata Estate, Craggy Range, Trinity Hill, Church Road and Stonecroft are all well-established names, several of them pioneers of the Gimblett Gravels.

What are the Gimblett Gravels?

A defined district within Hawke’s Bay whose deep, stony gravel soils radiate heat and drain freely. That warmth ripens and concentrates the reds, so it’s the region’s premier area for Bordeaux blends and Syrah.
