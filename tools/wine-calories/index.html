Wine Calories & Units: How Many Calories Are in a Glass of Wine?

A plain-language guide to what’s really in your glass — calories, alcohol units, and why dry and sweet wines differ.

“How many calories are in a glass of wine?” is one of the most-searched wine questions, and the honest answer is: it depends mostly on the alcohol. Wine calories come from two things — the alcohol and any leftover sugar — and for most everyday wines, alcohol does the heavy lifting.

As a friendly rule of thumb, a standard 150ml (5oz) glass of dry wine lands somewhere around 120 to 130 calories. Sweeter and stronger wines climb from there. Below you’ll find typical figures by wine style, plus a simple way to think about alcohol units so you always know roughly what you’re pouring.

No wrong answers here — this is just to help you (if you’re of legal drinking age) enjoy your glass with a bit more confidence.

What drives the calories in wine

Two things put calories in your glass: alcohol and sugar. Alcohol (ethanol) carries about 7 calories per gram — more than the 4 in a gram of sugar, and not far off pure fat — so the stronger the wine, the more calories it holds, even before any sweetness is added.

That’s why a bone-dry wine still has plenty of calories: it can have almost no sugar left, yet the alcohol alone accounts for most of the count. As a rough guide, every extra percentage point of ABV nudges the calories up. A 14.5% red will out-calorie a light 11% white, glass for glass.

The second factor is residual sugar — the natural grape sugar left over after fermentation. In dry wines there’s very little, so it barely registers. In sweet and dessert wines there can be a lot, and that’s where the numbers climb.

  • Alcohol: ~7 kcal per gram — the main driver in most wines.
  • Sugar: matters mostly in off-dry, sweet, and dessert wines.
  • Higher ABV = more calories, all else being equal.
  • Pour size matters just as much — a generous 250ml pour is nearly two standard glasses.

Dry vs sweet: why the numbers differ

“Dry” means most of the grape sugar has fermented into alcohol, leaving little sweetness behind. “Sweet” means some sugar was deliberately kept. This is the single biggest thing separating a ~120-calorie glass from a much richer one.

A dry red, dry white, dry rosé, or brut sparkling wine all sit in a similar band — roughly 115 to 130 calories per 150ml — because they’re mostly alcohol with negligible sugar. Move to an off-dry Riesling and you’ll add a little; move to a lusciously sweet dessert wine like Sauternes and the sugar can push a full glass well past 160 calories.

The friendly takeaway: if you’re watching calories, a dry wine at a moderate ABV is your lightest everyday choice — and sweet wines, which pack more per millilitre, are usually poured in smaller glasses anyway, which naturally keeps the total in check.

Alcohol units, explained simply

A “unit” is just a standard way to measure how much pure alcohol is in a drink, so you can compare a small strong pour with a large weaker one. In the UK, one unit is 10ml (about 8g) of pure alcohol.

The easy formula is: units = strength (ABV %) × volume (ml) ÷ 1000. So a 150ml glass of 13% wine is 13 × 150 ÷ 1000 = about 2 units. A large 250ml glass of the same wine is closer to 3.25 units — worth knowing, because restaurant pours are often larger than you’d guess.

Units are a helpful companion to calories: they track the alcohol, which is also the main calorie source. Keeping a rough eye on both is a simple way to stay in tune with what you’re drinking. (One note for context: the US uses a “standard drink” of about 14g of pure alcohol, so a US standard drink is roughly 1.75 UK units — the idea is the same, only the number differs.)

Small ways to enjoy your glass

None of this is about saying no to wine — it’s about knowing what’s in the glass so it fits comfortably into your day. A few gentle habits help: choose a slightly lower-ABV wine when you fancy something lighter, use a standard glass size rather than a generous free-pour, and enjoy a glass of water alongside. As with anything, it’s for adults of legal drinking age, and there’s no need to overdo it.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How many calories are in a glass of red wine?

A standard 150ml (5oz) glass of dry red wine at around 13-14% ABV has roughly 125 calories. Almost all of that comes from the alcohol, since dry reds contain very little residual sugar. A larger 250ml pour is closer to 210 calories.

Does white wine have fewer calories than red?

Usually just slightly. Many dry whites are a touch lower in alcohol than reds, which nudges them a little lower — around 120 calories per 150ml glass. But the difference is small; what matters most is the ABV and how big your pour is, not simply the colour.

Why is sweet wine higher in calories?

Sweet and dessert wines keep more of the grape’s natural sugar, and that sugar adds calories on top of the alcohol. A full 150ml glass can reach 160 calories or more. They’re often served in smaller glasses, though, which helps balance out the richer count.

How many units are in a glass of wine?

A standard 150ml glass of 13% wine is about 2 UK units. The quick formula is ABV × volume in ml ÷ 1000. A small 125ml glass is closer to 1.5 units, and a large 250ml restaurant pour is around 3.25 units.

Is wine fattening or bad for a diet?

Wine isn’t inherently “fattening,” but its calories are real and come mainly from alcohol. If you’re mindful of calories, choose a dry wine at a moderate ABV, stick to a standard glass size, and enjoy it as part of your overall balance rather than counting it as free.
