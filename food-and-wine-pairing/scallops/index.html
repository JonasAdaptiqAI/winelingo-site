What Wine Goes With Scallops?

The sweetest thing in the sea — and searing them changes the answer.

Chablis for raw or poached, a white Burgundy for seared, Champagne for either. Scallops are sweet, which is the thing most people miss.

Scallops contain more glycogen than most shellfish, which is why they taste distinctly sweet and why they caramelise so readily in a hot pan. That sweetness is the key to the pairing: a bone-dry, austere wine can taste sharp beside them, while the searing adds a browned, nutty layer that changes the answer again.

Raw and poached versus seared

Raw or lightly poached, a scallop is delicate, saline and sweet. It wants a wine that is mineral and precise rather than rich: Chablis, a Muscadet from Melon de Bourgogne, or Champagne.

Seared hard, the surface caramelises and the dish gains a nutty, browned character alongside whatever fat it was cooked in. That moves it towards a lightly oaked Chardonnay — a white Burgundy, where the oak meets the browning rather than fighting it.

The mistake in both directions is treating them as interchangeable. A big oaked white buries a raw scallop; a lean mineral white can seem thin beside a seared one in brown butter.

What to actually open

Chablis. The reference pairing for shellfish generally, and the safest single answer here. Unoaked Chardonnay with cutting acidity and a stony quality that suits saline sweetness.

White Burgundy with restrained oak for seared scallops, particularly with a butter or cream sauce. Meursault if you are being generous.

Champagne works with both, and better than either if the scallops are part of a mixed starter. A Blanc de Blancs — all Chardonnay — is the natural choice.

What is served with them

Black pudding or pancetta, a common British pairing, adds salt and fat and pushes towards the fuller white. Pea purée keeps it fresh and light and suits Chablis. A citrus or ceviche treatment raises the acidity bar and argues for Albariño or Assyrtiko.

Curry or chilli — scallops appear in both — moves the pairing to an off-dry Riesling, for the usual reasons about heat and alcohol.

Red is genuinely difficult. Shellfish and tannin produce a metallic taste, and scallops are too delicate to justify the attempt.

Common questions

What wine goes with scallops?

Chablis for raw or poached, a lightly oaked white Burgundy for seared, or Champagne for either.

Why do seared scallops need a different wine?

Searing caramelises their natural sugars and adds a nutty, browned layer. That suits a lightly oaked Chardonnay, where a lean mineral white can taste thin beside brown butter.

Are scallops really sweet?

Yes — they carry more glycogen than most shellfish, which is why they taste sweet and why they brown so readily. A very austere dry wine can taste sharp against them.

Can you drink red wine with scallops?

Best not. Shellfish and tannin together taste metallic, and scallops are delicate enough that even a light red overwhelms them.
