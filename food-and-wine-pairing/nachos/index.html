What Wine Goes With Nachos?

Salt, melted cheese and jalapeños. A pairing problem disguised as a snack.

Cava, a dry rosé, or a light Garnacha. Bubbles for the cheese, low alcohol for the chilli, cold for both.

Nachos are fried tortilla, melted cheese, salt and heat, usually with sour cream, guacamole and pickled jalapeños on top. Nobody thinks of it as a wine occasion, which is a shame, because the combination of fat and salt is one that sparkling wine handles better than almost anything else on a table.

Fat, salt and heat

Three mechanisms are in play and they conveniently point the same way. Fried tortilla and melted cheese coat the palate, and carbonation physically lifts that coating off in a way still wine cannot. Salt makes wine taste rounder and fruitier, so a sharp, inexpensive bottle shows better here than it does alone. And jalapeño heat is amplified by alcohol, which rules out anything strong.

The result is that a cheap dry sparkling wine, served very cold, is genuinely the right answer rather than a compromise.

What to actually open

Cava. Bottle-fermented like Champagne with at least nine months on the lees, which gives it a savoury, bready quality that suits melted cheese. Inexpensive enough to open without ceremony, which matters for nachos.

A dry rosé, well chilled. Fruit for the salsa and the beans, no tannin for the chilli to sharpen. A Spanish rosado is the natural pick.

Garnacha if the nachos are meat-heavy — beef chilli, chorizo. Keep it light and cool; a 15% Grenache will make the jalapeños twice as hot.

The toppings that change things

Guacamole is fatty and mild and reinforces the case for bubbles. Sour cream does the same. Pickled jalapeños bring vinegar as well as heat, and vinegar resets your palate to a very high acidity — which is one more reason to pick a wine with plenty of its own rather than something soft.

What to avoid: oaked Chardonnay, which combines with melted cheese into something heavy and cloying, and any tannic red, which the chilli will sharpen into bitterness.

Common questions

What wine goes with nachos?

Cava is the best answer — the bubbles cut fried tortilla and melted cheese, and the salt makes it taste fruitier. A dry rosé or a light Garnacha also work.

Why sparkling wine with nachos?

Carbonation physically lifts the fat coating off the palate, which still wine cannot do. Salt then softens the wine and makes it taste rounder, so an inexpensive bottle performs above its price.

What about the jalapeños?

They cap the alcohol. Capsaicin heat is amplified by alcohol and sharpened by tannin, so keep the wine light and cold — and note the pickling vinegar demands high acidity too.

Is red wine ever right with nachos?

Only if they are meat-heavy, and only a soft, light one served cool. A big Garnacha at 15% will make the chilli twice as hot.
