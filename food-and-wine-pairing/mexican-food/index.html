What Wine Goes With Mexican Food?

Lime, chilli, corn and coriander. Four things that push the wine in the same direction.

Albariño or Verdejo for anything with lime and fish; Garnacha for pork and beef; a dry rosé for a mixed table.

Mexican food covers everything from a plate of tacos to a mole that took two days to make. What runs through nearly all of it is lime, chilli, corn and fresh herbs, and those four set most of the wine rules before you get to the protein.

Lime and chilli, again

Lime is on almost every table, and acidity in food raises the bar the wine must clear — a wine less acidic than the plate tastes flat and hollow. Chilli caps the alcohol: capsaicin is amplified by alcohol and sharpened by tannin. Between them these two rule out most big, oaked, warm-climate reds.

What is left is a wide and genuinely enjoyable field: high-acid whites, dry rosés, sparkling wine, and light fruity reds served cool. Spanish wine works particularly well, which is convenient given the shared language on the label.

What to actually open

Albariño or Verdejo for ceviche, fish tacos, prawns and anything citrus-led. Saline, sharp and aromatic without being grassy — which matters, because coriander plus a herbaceous wine doubles the green rather than balancing it.

Garnacha for carnitas, al pastor, carne asada and barbacoa. Ripe red fruit and soft tannin meet charred pork and beef well. Pick a lighter bottling — Garnacha often runs hot, and alcohol works against the chilli.

Cava or a dry rosado when several dishes arrive at once, or when anything is fried. Carbonation lifts fat off the palate better than acidity alone.

Mole, and the sweet-savoury dishes

Mole poblano is the exception that needs its own thought: chocolate, chilli, nuts and dried fruit, savoury rather than sweet, and deeply complex. It is one of the few Mexican dishes that takes a fuller red — a Primitivo or a Zinfandel, whose brambly fruit meets the dried-fruit character. Watch the alcohol if there is real heat.

Anything with pineapple, sweet corn or a sweet salsa raises the same issue as barbecue: sugar in food makes a dry wine taste sharper, so lean towards riper fruit or a touch of residual sugar. An off-dry Riesling is a better Mexican wine than most people expect.

Common questions

What wine goes with Mexican food?

Albariño or Verdejo for lime and seafood dishes, Garnacha for pork and beef, and Cava or a dry rosado for a mixed table.

What wine goes with mole?

Mole is the exception that takes a fuller red — Primitivo or Zinfandel, whose brambly fruit meets the dried fruit and chocolate. Watch the alcohol if it is hot.

Does Sauvignon Blanc work with Mexican food?

Less well than you would think. Fresh coriander already brings a green, pungent edge, and a grassy Sauvignon doubles it. Albariño is aromatic without being herbaceous.

Why avoid big reds with Mexican food?

Lime raises the acidity the wine has to match, and chilli heat is amplified by alcohol and sharpened by tannin. Big oaked reds lose on both counts.
