What Wine Goes With Brie or Camembert?

Soft, buttery, bloomy-rinded cheeses — where the rind, not the cheese, decides the wine.

Bubbles. Champagne or Crémant is the answer nine times out of ten, and it is not a cop-out — it is the pairing these cheeses were practically designed for.

What makes Brie and Camembert interesting to pair is that you are matching two things at once: the rich butterfat of the paste, and the earthy, mushroomy white rind. Get the second one wrong and the wine turns unpleasantly metallic.

Two ways to solve it

Cut through the fat. Soft cheeses coat your palate with butterfat, and something acidic and sparkling scrubs it clean between bites. Champagne and Crémant do this beautifully, and their toasty lees character happens to echo the rind at the same time. A crisp unoaked Chardonnay achieves the same thing with acidity alone.

Or echo the rind. A light, low-tannin Pinot Noir works by agreeing with the cheese rather than cutting against it — its forest-floor and red-berry notes chime with the mushroomy rind. This is the more interesting pairing when it lands.

The metallic problem

Here is the thing most pairing charts skip. The white bloom on these cheeses is a Penicillium mould, and it reacts badly with firm red tannin — the combination can taste distinctly metallic, almost like sucking a coin.

So the rule is not "no red wine with soft cheese", which is the advice you will often see. It is no tannic red. A silky Pinot Noir with barely any grip is genuinely lovely here. A structured Cabernet Sauvignon is the thing that ruins it.

If you are baking it

A whole baked Camembert is a different dish and wants a different wine. Heat makes the paste molten and mellows the rind's sharpness, and by the time you have added garlic, honey or rosemary the cheese is no longer the loudest thing on the plate.

Baked, it can take something with a bit more weight — a fuller white, or a light red served slightly cool. If honey is involved, an off-dry white is suddenly a very good idea; our wine sweetness chart shows where those sit.

FAQ

Questions & answers

What wine goes best with Brie?

Champagne or Crémant. The bubbles and acidity cut through the butterfat, and their toasty lees character echoes the mushroomy rind. A light, low-tannin Pinot Noir is the best red option.

Can you drink red wine with Brie or Camembert?

Yes, but keep it light and low in tannin. The Penicillium rind reacts with firm tannins and can taste metallic, so a silky Pinot Noir works well while a structured Cabernet Sauvignon does not.

What wine goes with baked Camembert?

Baking mellows the rind and makes the cheese richer, so it takes a fuller white or a lightly chilled red. If you have baked it with honey, an off-dry white is a particularly good match.
