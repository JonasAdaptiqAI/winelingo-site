What Wine Goes With Shepherd’s Pie?

Lamb, gravy and mash. The gravy is what you are actually pairing with.

Syrah, Malbec, or a Rioja Crianza. Medium to full, savoury rather than jammy, and not too fine — this dish will flatten a delicate wine.

Shepherd’s pie is minced lamb in a thickened gravy under a lid of mashed potato, usually browned on top. Two features drive the pairing and neither is the lamb itself: the gravy, which is savoury, salty and often carries Worcestershire sauce or tomato purée, and the mash, which is starchy enough to dull the palate.

Starch mutes wine

A thick layer of buttery mashed potato coats the tongue and blunts your perception of everything that follows. It is the same reason bread is served at a tasting — starch resets and dampens. Against that, a delicate wine simply disappears; you taste the first sip and then nothing.

This is the argument for a wine with real presence. Not necessarily a big one, but one with enough fruit concentration and savoury depth to still register through a mouthful of potato.

Pair with the gravy, not the meat

Lamb on its own suits a wide range of reds. Lamb in a dark, salty, umami-rich gravy narrows it considerably: the salt softens the wine and makes it taste fruitier, while the umami sharpens tannin. A very tannic young red will taste hard here even though lamb usually welcomes tannin.

So the target is a wine with medium tannin and a savoury rather than sweet character. Syrah from the northern Rhône or a cooler part of Australia gives you black pepper and cured-meat savouriness that meets the gravy directly. Malbec is the crowd-pleasing choice, plush and dark. Rioja Crianza is arguably the best of the three: Tempranillo with a little age and gentle oak, which brings a savoury, leathery quality that suits browned mince better than bright young fruit.

And cottage pie

The same dish made with beef is cottage pie, and the distinction is worth respecting — shepherd’s pie is lamb, by definition. Beef mince is less fatty and less distinctly flavoured, which lets you drop to something a bit lighter: a Chianti or a Montepulciano works well.

If the top is heavily browned or has cheese in it, that char and salt argue for a little more fruit and a little less tannin again — the Malbec end of the range rather than the Syrah end.

Common questions

What wine goes with shepherd’s pie?

Syrah, Malbec or a Rioja Crianza. Medium to full bodied, savoury rather than jammy, and with enough presence to survive the mashed potato.

Why does the mash matter?

Starch coats the tongue and blunts flavour — it is why bread is served at tastings. A delicate wine disappears entirely under a layer of buttery mash, so the wine needs real concentration.

Should I pair with the lamb or the gravy?

The gravy. It is salty and umami-rich, which softens the wine but sharpens tannin, so a very tannic young red tastes harder here than lamb alone would suggest.

Is cottage pie the same pairing?

Close, but beef mince is leaner and milder than lamb, so you can go a bit lighter — a Chianti or Montepulciano suits it well.
