What Wine Goes With Quiche?

A savoury custard in pastry. The eggs decide this, not the filling.

Crémant d’Alsace, an Alsace Pinot Blanc, or Chablis. Unoaked and high in acid, and bubbles if you have them.

Quiche is a custard tart: eggs and cream set in pastry, with whatever else is folded through. That makes it a harder pairing than it looks, because eggs are the single most difficult common food to match with wine — and the reason is mechanical rather than a matter of taste.

Why eggs are the problem

Set egg custard is an emulsion of fat and protein that coats the tongue and stays there. That coating blunts everything after it, so a subtle wine is simply erased — you taste the first sip and then nothing. Egg also carries sulphur compounds, which react badly with the sulphur notes in oaked or reductive wines and can turn faintly metallic.

The wine that survives needs high acidity to cut the coating and no oak to react with it. Add the pastry — butter and starch, which dull the palate further — and the specification narrows to something sharp, unoaked and preferably sparkling.

What to actually open

Crémant d’Alsace. Made the same way as Champagne with a second fermentation in the bottle, at a fraction of the price. Carbonation is the one thing that physically lifts an egg coating off the palate, which no still wine manages. Alsace is also the right neighbourhood — quiche is from Lorraine, next door.

Alsace Pinot Blanc. The quiet regional answer: dry, appley, unoaked, with enough texture to sit beside cream without competing. If you can find a Sylvaner, that works too.

Chablis. Unoaked Chardonnay with the acidity to cut custard and enough weight not to vanish under pastry. The safest supermarket answer on this page.

Quiche Lorraine, and the rest

Proper quiche Lorraine is eggs, cream and lardons — no cheese at all, which is a later addition. The bacon adds salt, and salt makes wine taste rounder and fruitier, so a lean high-acid white fills out beside it rather than tasting thin.

Spinach and cheese pushes it greener and saltier: Sauvignon Blanc or a Grüner Veltliner. Mushroom makes it earthier and is the one version where a very light red works — a chilled Gamay, nothing with grip.

Red is otherwise the thing to avoid. Tannin against egg yolk tastes metallic, and there is no fat-and-protein structure here of the sort that lets tannin do its job.

Common questions

What wine goes with quiche?

A Crémant d’Alsace, an Alsace Pinot Blanc, or Chablis — all unoaked and high in acid, which is what the egg custard demands.

What is the best white wine with quiche?

Chablis if you want a still wine, Crémant d’Alsace if you want bubbles. Both are unoaked; an oaked Chardonnay reacts badly with the sulphur compounds in egg.

Does red wine go with quiche?

Rarely. Tannin and egg yolk together taste metallic, and there is nothing here for tannin to bind to. A mushroom quiche is the exception — a chilled Gamay works.

What wine goes with quiche Lorraine?

The same answer, helped by the bacon. Salt makes wine taste rounder and fruitier, so a lean high-acid white like Crémant or Chablis fills out beside lardons rather than tasting thin.
