What Wine Goes With Korean Food?

Fermented chilli paste, sesame and something pickled on every table.

An off-dry Riesling, or a chilled Gamay for red. Fruit and freshness — the fermented sides out-acid anything soft.

Korean food runs on gochujang and gochugaru — fermented chilli paste and chilli flakes — with sesame, garlic, soy and something pickled on the table at every meal. That combination brings heat, deep umami and sharp acidity all at once, which is a harder problem than heat alone.

Three things at once

Heat. Capsaicin is amplified by alcohol and sharpened by tannin, so big strong reds make the food hotter and themselves harder.

Umami. Gochujang, doenjang and soy are all fermented and dense with glutamates, which sharpen tannin further and hollow out fruit.

Acidity. Kimchi and the banchan are properly sour, which raises the bar the wine must clear — a soft, low-acid wine tastes flabby beside them.

Low alcohol, low tannin, high acidity, a little sweetness. That is a narrow target and it is almost exactly Riesling.

What to actually open

Off-dry Riesling. The sugar damps the gochujang, the acidity meets the kimchi, and the low alcohol keeps the burn down. The most reliable answer across the whole cuisine.

Chilled Gamay. A Beaujolais cru is the red answer, and a good one for Korean barbecue: bright fruit for the sweet marinade, high acidity, almost no tannin. Serve it cool — it matters more than which bottle.

Cava or a dry rosé for a table with a lot of fried items — twigim, pajeon — where carbonation earns its place.

By dish

Bulgogi and galbi are marinated with soy, sugar, garlic and pear, so they are sweet as well as savoury — see Korean BBQ for the full argument, but chilled Gamay or off-dry Riesling both work.

Bibimbap with a spoonful of gochujang is moderately hot and heavy on vegetables and sesame; Riesling handles it. Jjigae — the bubbling stews — are the hottest and most fermented things on most menus, and want the lowest-alcohol wine you have, well chilled.

Korean fried chicken deserves its own note: it is sweet, sticky, fried and often very hot, which is the exact case for sparkling. Cava, cold, and do not overthink it.

Common questions

What wine goes with Korean food?

An off-dry Riesling is the most reliable answer, or a chilled Gamay if you want red. Low alcohol, low tannin, high acidity.

Why is Korean food harder than just spicy food?

Because three things happen at once: chilli heat, deep umami from fermented pastes that sharpens tannin, and genuinely sour banchan that out-acid a soft wine.

What wine goes with Korean fried chicken?

Cava or another dry sparkling, served cold. It is sweet, sticky, fried and often hot — carbonation cuts the fat and the bubbles handle the glaze.

Should I chill the red wine?

Yes, and it matters more than which bottle you choose. Twenty minutes in the fridge turns a light red from soft and alcoholic into fresh and fruity, which is what hot, sweet food needs.
