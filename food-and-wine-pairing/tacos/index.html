What Wine Goes With Tacos?

Lime, coriander, chilli and whatever is in the tortilla. The garnishes matter as much as the meat.

Albariño for fish tacos, Garnacha for pork or beef, a dry rosé for a mixed table. High acid, low tannin, served cold.

Taco is not one dish. Fish, carnitas, al pastor, carne asada and barbacoa are different pairings, and the fillings vary more than most cuisines. But three things are nearly always present — lime, chilli and raw onion or coriander — and those three set most of the rules before you get to the meat.

The garnishes rule first

Lime raises the acidity bar sharply. A wine less acidic than the food tastes flabby and hollow, so anything soft and low-acid is out regardless of what is in the tortilla. Chilli caps the alcohol: capsaicin is amplified by alcohol and sharpened by tannin, so big, strong reds make the burn worse and themselves taste harder.

Coriander and raw onion add a green, pungent edge. That argues against a very herbaceous wine — grassy Sauvignon Blanc plus a fistful of coriander is a doubling rather than a match — and towards something aromatic but fruit-driven.

What to actually open

Fish tacos: Albariño. From Rías Baixas — high acid, saline, stone-fruited. It handles the lime, the white fish and the chipotle mayonnaise without effort. A Verdejo does much the same job.

Carnitas, al pastor, carne asada: Garnacha. Ripe red fruit, soft tannin, and enough generosity to meet charred pork and pineapple. Check the alcohol and pick a lighter bottling; serve it cool.

A mixed table: dry rosé. When four fillings arrive at once, the wine that is good with all of them beats the one that is perfect with one. A Spanish rosado or a Cava — the bubbles also handle anything fried.

Heat, and what to avoid

If the salsa is serious — habanero, or a heavy hand with the chipotle — drop the alcohol and chill harder. Sweetness helps too, so an off-dry Riesling is a genuinely good taco wine even though nobody expects it.

Avoid oaked reds and oaked whites alike. Oak plus lime turns bitter, and oak plus chilli reads as heavy and slightly medicinal. And avoid anything tannic: with lime, chilli and often fish in the same bite, tannin has nothing to bind to and everything to clash with.

Common questions

What wine goes with tacos?

Albariño for fish tacos, Garnacha for pork or beef, and a dry rosé or Cava when several fillings are on the table.

What is the best wine with fish tacos?

Albariño. It has the acidity for the lime, the salinity for the fish, and enough fruit for a smoky chipotle sauce — without the tannin that would clash.

Can I drink red wine with tacos?

Yes, but keep it soft and cool. Garnacha with low tannin works well with carnitas or carne asada. Tannic, oaked reds fight the lime and sharpen the chilli.

What wine works if the salsa is very hot?

An off-dry Riesling. Residual sugar damps capsaicin heat, and its low alcohol avoids making the burn worse.
