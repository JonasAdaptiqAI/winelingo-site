What Wine Goes With Lasagne (Lasagna)?

Ragù, béchamel and pasta in layers. The white sauce changes the answer.

Sangiovese, Barbera, or a dry Lambrusco. High acidity is compulsory; heavy tannin is not welcome.

Lasagne alla bolognese comes from Emilia-Romagna: layers of pasta, slow-cooked ragù and béchamel, baked until the top browns. It is richer than a plain tomato pasta and gentler than it looks, because the béchamel softens everything — including, unhelpfully, the wine.

Béchamel is why this differs from tomato pasta

A thick dairy sauce coats the palate and makes firm tannin taste dusty and drying rather than structured. At the same time the ragù is savoury and meaty, and the tomato in it — much less than most people use, in the classic version — is acidic. So you want a wine with high acidity and only moderate tannin, which is a narrower target than either sauce alone would suggest.

That combination describes the local wines almost exactly, which is not a coincidence. Emilia-Romagna produces sharp, food-shaped reds because that is what its food asks for.

What to actually open

Dry Lambrusco is the genuine local answer and still the most surprising one to anybody who met the sweet export version in the 1980s. Proper Lambrusco is dry, faintly frothy and sharply acidic, and the bubbles cut baked cheese and béchamel better than any still wine. Look for secco on the label.

Sangiovese. Sour cherry, dried herbs and enough acidity to cut through the richness. A Chianti Classico is the easy version; a Sangiovese di Romagna is the closer one geographically.

Barbera. Higher acidity again and naturally low tannin, which suits a béchamel-heavy lasagne better than Sangiovese does.

What is in your layers

A vegetable lasagne — courgette, spinach, mushroom — drops the meat and with it the need for structure. Go lighter still: a Valpolicella or a chilled Gamay, or even a textured white like Verdicchio.

A heavily tomato-led ragù, as most home versions are, pushes the acidity requirement up and makes Barbera the safer pick. And the browned cheese on top adds salt, which softens whatever you open and lets you get away with a sharper wine than you might expect.

What to avoid: Cabernet Sauvignon and anything heavily oaked. The tannin is stripped bare by the béchamel and the oak collides with the tomato.

Common questions

What wine goes with lasagne?

Sangiovese or Barbera for a still red, or a dry Lambrusco for the genuine Emilia-Romagna answer. All are high in acid and moderate in tannin.

What is the best wine for lasagna?

Barbera, if you want one answer. It has the highest acidity of the common Italian reds and the least tannin, which suits the béchamel better than a firmer wine.

Can I drink red wine with lasagne?

Yes — red is the right direction. Just keep the tannin moderate. A thick dairy sauce makes firm tannin taste dusty, so Cabernet Sauvignon and anything heavily oaked are the wrong choices.

Is Lambrusco really a good match?

It is the local one, and dry Lambrusco is nothing like the sweet export version. It is sharp and faintly frothy, and the bubbles cut baked cheese and béchamel better than any still wine. Look for secco.
