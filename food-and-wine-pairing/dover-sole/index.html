What Wine Goes With Dover Sole?

The most delicate fish there is. The whole problem is not drowning it.

By Jonas Egeskov · Updated 6 September 2026

Chablis or a restrained white Burgundy. Precision rather than power — this is the most easily overwhelmed dish in the section.

Dover sole is the finest of the flatfish: firm, sweet, subtly flavoured, and expensive enough that it is usually cooked simply — meunière, in butter with lemon and parsley, or grilled on the bone. There is nothing here to fight a wine. The entire risk is that the wine buries the fish.

Restraint is the whole skill

Most pairings ask what in the food will attack the glass. Sole asks the opposite question: how little wine can you get away with? Its flavour is delicate and slightly sweet, and anything aromatic, oaked or high in alcohol will simply talk over it.

What it needs is a wine with good acidity to cut the butter, enough texture not to seem thin, and aromatics that stay quiet. That is a narrow and rather classical specification, which is why the traditional answers here have not changed in a century.

What to actually open

Chablis. Unoaked Chardonnay, stony and precise, with the acidity to handle beurre noisette. Village level is right; there is no need to go further up.

A restrained white Burgundy. Meursault or Saint-Aubin with light oak, if the fish is served with a richer butter sauce. Anything heavily toasted is too much.

Muscadet from Melon de Bourgogne, or a Sancerre if you want something sharper. Both are lean enough to stay out of the way.

Meunière, grilled and the sauces

Sole meunière is floured, fried in butter and finished with lemon and browned butter. The nuttiness of beurre noisette is the one argument for a touch of oak — a lightly oaked white Burgundy is the classic match, and it is classic for a reason.

Grilled on the bone and served plain, it is even more delicate: go to Chablis or Muscadet and no further.

Sole véronique, with grapes and a cream sauce, adds sweetness — an off-dry Vouvray handles that better than a bone-dry wine would.

What to avoid: Sauvignon Blanc at its grassiest, aromatic varieties like Gewürztraminer, and anything at 14% or above. All three drown the fish.

Common questions

What wine goes with Dover sole?

Chablis for a plainly cooked fish, or a restrained white Burgundy if there is a butter sauce. Precision matters more than power.

Why is Dover sole so easy to overwhelm?

Its flavour is delicate and slightly sweet, and it is usually cooked very simply. Anything aromatic, oaked or high in alcohol talks straight over it.

Does oaked wine ever work with sole?

Lightly oaked, with sole meunière — the nuttiness of browned butter meets a little oak well. Anything heavily toasted is too much.

What should I avoid?

Grassy Sauvignon Blanc, aromatic varieties like Gewürztraminer, and anything at 14% alcohol or above. All three drown the fish.
